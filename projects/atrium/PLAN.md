# atrium — PLAN

> The portfolio's front door: a single, scroll-driven cinematic landing page that presents the six showcase projects as the lobby of the whole portfolio.
> Brand display: **Atrium**. Repo dir: `atrium`. Portfolio slot 7 (the landing page itself).

## Problem

A portfolio of seven production-grade showcases has, today, no front door. A recruiter or senior engineer who receives the link arrives at a bare README table or jumps straight into one project with no framing — no sense of the range (four distinct backends, two creative web-only showcases), no narrative, no single arresting first impression that says "this person can art-direct a page as well as they can architect a system." `atrium` is that front door. It is the **lobby**: one page that frames the six projects, tells the portfolio's range-and-craft story, and — because every project in this portfolio earns its existence with a wow moment — is itself the strongest visual piece in the collection. It presents the six showcases as cinematic, scroll-revealed sections/cards, each with a real pitch and two links out (live Fly demo + GitHub repo), and it is itself project #7: a web-only, GSAP-led scroll experience.

## Audience

A single, sharply-defined audience, and the design is tuned entirely for them — there is no second "real user" fiction to balance (unlike `razors-edge`/`apex`):

- **The recruiter / hiring manager / senior engineer evaluating Jan Antczak.** They arrive at the portfolio root URL from a CV link, a LinkedIn/GitHub profile, a referral DM, or an Awwwards-style roundup. **Primary context is desktop** — a 1440 px+ monitor (1920 px common), a decent GPU, and a habit of opening DevTools and reading source. They give the page **five to ten seconds** to decide whether this is "another Next.js template portfolio" or "someone whose front door alone is worth a closer look." They scroll. They want, fast: (1) an immediate signal of craft (the wow moment, before any copy), (2) a scannable sense of the six projects — what each is, what stack, what's impressive about it — and (3) a frictionless path out to the thing they want to inspect (the live demo, the repo). A meaningful minority arrive on **mobile** (a link opened on a phone from LinkedIn/Slack), so 320 px-up is a first-class target, not an afterthought — but the art direction is tuned for the desktop scroll. They are time-poor, sceptical, and have seen a hundred portfolios this month; the page must reward the scroll and never waste their time hunting for a link.

The whole IA optimises for "understand the range in one scroll, then leave for the project you care about." The wow moment buys the five seconds; the project sections spend them well; the links out are unmissable.

## Wow moment

**A scroll-driven cinematic "lobby" sequence: the word `ATRIUM` is built in light on a near-black field, then — as the viewer scrolls — the camera appears to descend through the wordmark into an architectural atrium of light, and each of the six projects arrives as a pinned, kinetic "gallery bay" that the scroll glides past, the project's name set in large kinetic type that resolves from its stack-coloured signature into a legible title as its bay locks into frame.**

Concretely, through the scroll:

- **First paint (zero scroll):** a near-black field with a single warm volumetric shaft of light, and the wordmark **ATRIUM** set in a confident architectural display face, centre-stage, the letters lit as if by that shaft. One line of supporting copy ("The portfolio of Jan Antczak — six showcases, four backends, one quality bar.") and a quiet scroll affordance. The whole first viewport reads as the threshold of a building you are about to walk into. A barely-perceptible idle drift of the light shaft breathes the scene (disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`).
- **Descent (first viewport of scroll):** a GSAP `ScrollTrigger` pins the hero and scrubs a "descent" — the `ATRIUM` wordmark scales and parallaxes as if the viewer drops through the letterforms into the space beyond, the single shaft of light opening into the colonnade of an atrium (built from CSS/SVG gradients + layered parallax planes, **not** WebGL). The descent resolves into the first project bay, so the scroll never stalls on a finished animation — the descent _is_ the transition into the gallery.
- **The six gallery bays (the spine):** each project is a **pinned bay** the scroll glides through. As a bay locks into frame, the project's name resolves with kinetic typography (a weight/tracking settle, or a wipe from its stack-signature colour into legible title type), the one-line pitch reveals, a stack ribbon and a representative motif animate in, and the two outward links (Live demo / GitHub) settle into a fixed, unmissable position. The connective tissue between bays is a recurring **"shaft of light / threshold"** motif — each transition reads as moving from one lit bay of the atrium to the next. The six bays are deliberately differentiated in accent treatment (each project carries a signature hue drawn from its own identity — tape's slate/cyan, atlas's control-room green, apex's premium chrome, etc.) so the scroll reads as a tour of distinct rooms, not six identical cards.
- **The arrival / index (resolution):** the descent and the six bays resolve into a final "directory" state — a calm, fully-legible index of all six projects (a grid/list) plus the portfolio's range statement and the author's contact, so a viewer who wants to skip the cinema can reach every link immediately. The cinema is additive over a directory that always works.

This is the spine of the design, not decoration: the "atrium / shaft of light / descent-through-type / bay-to-bay threshold" motif is the connective tissue, and the six signature hues make the range _visible_ as you scroll. Reference points from `docs/inspirations.md`: **Olivier Larose** (scroll-driven storytelling, pinned sequential reveals — the closest analogue to the bay-to-bay tour), **Stripe** (long-form scrolling page where a custom scroll element justifies its bundle; type as a primary design element), **Aristide Benoist** (kinetic/generative typography for the wordmark and the per-bay title resolves), **Linear** + **Klim** (the directory chrome restraint, the display type held as a specimen). The designer-critic judges the hero descent and the bay sequence against **Olivier Larose** + **Stripe** specifically, with zero pochwał.

**Distinct from `razors-edge` (the accepted GSAP overlap).** `razors-edge` is a _cinematic dark-luxe barbershop_ whose motif is a **straight-razor blade slicing a wordmark** to reveal a portrait — a single hero cut, warm brass-on-black, photographic. `atrium`'s motif is an **architectural descent through type into a colonnade of light, then a sequential tour of six differently-lit bays** — a multi-stage spatial journey, cooler and more structural, type- and light-driven rather than photographic, and built around _sequencing six distinct things_ rather than _one hero reveal_. They share only the tool (GSAP ScrollTrigger), per the owner's explicit call. The designer-critic must confirm `atrium` does not read as a razors-edge re-skin: different motif (architecture/light vs blade/portrait), different palette strategy (six signature hues vs one brass accent), different structure (sequential six-bay tour vs single hero cut).

**Defined degradation is part of the spec, not an afterthought** (see "Accessibility" and the three-tier contract below): under `prefers-reduced-motion` the descent and bay pins are fully neutralised to a static, readable, scroll-normal page; under no-JS the page renders as the complete directory with all six projects and all twelve links present in real DOM.

## Stack flavour

**`web-only`.** Owner-confirmed and correct against `docs/conventions.md` § 10. None of the five api-heavy triggers fires:

- **No WebSocket / SSE.** Nothing streams. The page is a static, scroll-driven index of six projects.
- **No background jobs / queue / cron.** There is no server-side scheduled work whatsoever.
- **API not consumed by a non-Next client.** There is no API. The only data is a typed, in-repo project-config module (`src/data/projects.ts`) read at build/render time by this Next app and nothing else.
- **No heavy auth flow.** No auth at all — no accounts, no sessions, no tokens. It is a public landing page.
- **No heavy domain logic worth isolating from the UI.** There is essentially no domain logic — the "data" is a fixed array of six project descriptors. There is nothing to test in isolation from the UI beyond the typed config's shape (a Zod parse), which lives perfectly well in `src/lib/`.

`api-heavy` is rejected on need grounds: forcing a backend onto a static showcase index would be exactly the ceremony CLAUDE.md § 3 forbids. **Portfolio-composition note (planner enforcement, `docs/conventions.md` § 12):** the constraint is already satisfied with wide margin — the existing six projects include **four api-heavy across four distinct backends** (tape Elysia/Bun, meld Hono/Node, pulse NestJS, atlas Fastify) plus two web-only creative showcases (razors-edge, apex). `atrium` as slot 7 web-only does **not** threaten compliance (4 api-heavy of 7, all four § 11 backends represented). There is no open backend axis for `atrium` to fill, and a backend here would not advance variance — it would only add ceremony. The web-only call is therefore not drift; it is the only honest flavour for a portfolio landing page. See DECISIONS.md ADR-001.

## Animation stack — GSAP (ScrollTrigger + timeline)

**Selected: GSAP 3.x with the ScrollTrigger plugin as the single primary animation library**, per `docs/conventions.md` § 15 and CLAUDE.md § 5 (one library, do not stack three). This is owner-confirmed.

Justification against the § 15 decision criteria:

- The wow moment is a **scroll-driven, pinned, scrubbed, multi-stage timeline**: a hero descent that hands off into a **sequence of six pinned bays**, each with its own kinetic-type resolve and threshold transition, choreographed against a single scroll progress with precise hand-offs between stages. This is the textbook § 15 case where "Motion's declarative model fights you" — sequential pinned bays with per-bay mid-timeline state changes (the type resolve at lock-in) and clean stage-to-stage hand-offs are exactly what ScrollTrigger exists for. `razors-edge` already proved this exact pattern (pinned hero + pinned horizontal gallery + refresh-coordinator) in this codebase; `atrium` reuses the _technique_ and the hard-won integration lessons, not the visual identity.
- The recurring "shaft of light / threshold" transitions, the kinetic-type title resolves, and the parallax descent planes all benefit from GSAP's timeline + transform strengths (§ 15: "complex choreography with many independently animated elements").
- The architectural-light surface does **not** need WebGL — R3F is explicitly rejected (§ 15: R3F is "not for fancy 2D backgrounds — those should be CSS or Motion"; here the light/atrium is CSS/SVG gradients + 2D parallax planes). Keeping it off the GPU-3D path protects the Lighthouse ≥ 95 budget on mid-tier mobile, which matters doubly because **this is the page most likely to be opened first and judged hardest.**

**Motion is permitted only as a scoped secondary for small React-state micro-interactions** where GSAP would be overkill — specifically: the theme-toggle crossfade, hover/active states on the directory cards and link buttons, and any small mount transition (a copied-email toast, a focus-trap menu). This is the same bounded posture `razors-edge` ratified: **no scroll-driven animation may be authored in Motion; no scroll/pin/scrub/timeline work leaves GSAP.** Motion's `useScroll`/`useTransform` are banned. Crucially — and unlike razors-edge, which has a five-step wizard that justified Motion's `AnimatePresence` — `atrium` has **no presence-heavy interactive surface**, so the bar for Motion earning its bundle is higher: the preferred end state is **GSAP-only with CSS for hover/theme**, and the architect should default to that in ADR-002 unless a specific micro-interaction provably needs Motion's presence/layout tracking. Hover and theme crossfade default to CSS. (Per `docs/conventions.md` § 15, the architect ratifies the exact split in ADR-002; if Motion earns nothing, the project ships single-library, which is the ideal here.)

## Design direction

Sovereign visual identity — **no token reuse from any sibling project** (`docs/conventions.md` § 14, ADR-001). Built from scratch in `app/globals.css`. This is load-bearing: a portfolio landing page that borrowed `razors-edge`'s brass-on-black or `apex`'s palette would collapse exactly the "variance is the point" thesis the page exists to prove.

- **Aesthetic: architectural, cinematic, structural.** A near-black/deep-charcoal field with **volumetric warm light** as the signature (the "atrium" is a space made of light), generous negative space, large architectural display type, atmosphere over density. Premium and arresting — explicitly not "clean minimal because it's safe." The atrium-of-light metaphor gives the page a cooler, more structural register than razors-edge's warm photographic luxe.
- **Six signature accent hues — the range made visible.** Atrium's own chrome is near-monochrome (light-on-near-black + one neutral warm accent for the wordmark/threshold), but **each project bay borrows a single signature hue from that project's own identity** (e.g. tape slate/cyan, atlas control-room green, apex premium chrome/blue, meld collaborative warm, pulse status green/amber, razors-edge brass). These hues are atrium-local tokens (not imported from the sibling projects — § 14), hand-picked to evoke each project while staying inside atrium's contrast and harmony rules. The scroll thus reads as a tour through six differently-lit rooms — the single strongest way to _show_ (not tell) the portfolio's range.
- **Dark is the canonical default; the light theme is intentional, not an inversion** (CLAUDE.md § 4). Ship both via CSS variables + `next-themes`, dark default, `prefers-color-scheme` respected, no FOUC. The light theme is a deliberate "daytime atrium / architectural daylight" register — a warm bone/limestone field, charcoal ink, the warm accent and the six signature hues re-tuned to hold contrast on light — designed so a recruiter toggling the theme sees craft in both, not a broken dark-site-with-the-colours-flipped. The six signature hues must be contrast-verified in _both_ themes.
- **Type as a primary design element** (Stripe / Klim references). An architectural display face for the wordmark and the per-bay project titles (variable preferred so weight/width can animate on the kinetic title resolves — references the `font-variation-settings` note in `docs/inspirations.md`), plus a clean sans for UI and the project pitches. The wordmark must hold up as a specimen, not just a logo.
- **Light, not photography, is the texture.** Unlike razors-edge (film-grain photographic), atrium's surface is built from **volumetric-light gradients, soft god-ray shafts, and architectural parallax planes** (CSS/SVG, NOT WebGL), with at most a very subtle noise/grain to prevent banding on the large dark gradients. There is no photographic art-direction dependency — which also keeps the page light and fast. Optionally, each bay may show a small, optimised representative still/preview of its project (an `next/image` AVIF thumbnail of the actual showcase), but the design must not _depend_ on screenshots being present (they are an enhancement over the type-and-light composition, and they are a real-asset-sourcing task, not a blocker).

The designer-critic reviews every UI milestone against `docs/inspirations.md` with zero pochwał — see Phase 5, and the explicit "is this a razors-edge re-skin?" gate.

## Project-data model (the single typed source of truth)

All six projects' presentation data lives in **one typed, validated config module** so links, pitches, stacks, and accents are never hardcoded inline and the GitHub-link strategy has exactly one seam to flip when a remote exists.

- **`src/data/projects.ts`** — exports a typed, ordered `PROJECTS` array (the canonical portfolio order: tape, meld, razors-edge, pulse, apex, atlas — matching the root README/PROGRESS slots 1–6). Each entry is a `Project` shaped (and Zod-validated at module load, `src/lib/schemas/project.ts`) roughly:
  - `{ slug, name, tagline, pitch, category: 'api-heavy' | 'web-only', backend?: 'Elysia (Bun)' | 'Hono (Node)' | 'NestJS' | 'Fastify' | null, stack: string[], accentToken, wowMoment, demoUrl, repoUrl, previewImage?, year }`.
  - **`pitch`** is the recruiter-facing one/two-line description sourced from the root `README.md` project table (the source of truth for exact pitches/stacks). `wowMoment` is the one-line "what holds you for five seconds" per project.
  - **`demoUrl`** is the project's real public Fly URL (e.g. `https://tape-demo.fly.dev`) — linked **normally**; the page never surfaces the internal "Fly stopped to control cost" status (the demos auto-start on URL hit / restart on request; the public URL stays normal per owner policy). Do not add any "demo may be sleeping" wording.
  - **`repoUrl`** is derived from a single `GITHUB_BASE` constant (see below) — never a hardcoded inline URL.
  - **`accentToken`** names the bay's signature-hue CSS variable (the atrium-local token, not a sibling import).
- **`GITHUB_BASE` constant + the no-remote-yet strategy.** The repo currently has **no git remote** (CLAUDE.md § 10), so per-repo GitHub links cannot be real today. `src/data/projects.ts` (or a small `src/lib/site-config.ts`) defines a single `GITHUB_BASE` constant (e.g. `const GITHUB_BASE = process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_GITHUB_BASE ?? 'https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>'` — a documented placeholder), and each project's `repoUrl` is computed from it (e.g. `${GITHUB_BASE}/tree/main/projects/<slug>` for a monorepo layout, or `${GITHUB_BASE}-<slug>` if the owner later splits repos — the architect picks the shape in ADR-001/an ADR and documents it). **The moment a remote exists, flipping `GITHUB_BASE` (one constant / one env var) makes all six repo links real.** Until then the placeholder is honest and centralised — there are zero hardcoded broken `github.com/...` strings scattered in components. How the placeholder presents in the UI before it is real (a disabled "repo" affordance vs a live link to the placeholder) is a small design call recorded in AGENT_NOTES and decided at implementation; the data-model requirement is that it is a single seam.
- **Why a typed module, not a CMS or fetch:** six fixed entries, read at build time, validated by Zod for shape safety. This is the web-only thesis — there is nothing to fetch and nothing to administer.

## Site sections / IA

Single long-form scrolling landing page (no sub-routes required for v1; an optional `/#<slug>` anchor per project), in this order:

1. **Hero / threshold** — the wow moment first paint: the `ATRIUM` wordmark in the shaft of light, the portfolio one-liner, the scroll affordance, and a persistent quiet header that appears after the hero (brand mark, anchor jumps to the six projects + the directory, theme toggle, and a "View all" / contact affordance).
2. **The descent** — the pinned GSAP scrub from the wordmark into the atrium-of-light, handing off into the first project bay. (Not a separate content section — it is the hero-to-gallery transition.)
3. **Six project bays** — one pinned, kinetic bay per project, in portfolio order, each rendering from `PROJECTS[i]`: the resolving kinetic title, the pitch, the stack ribbon (with a clear `api-heavy · <backend>` or `web-only (creative)` badge), the project's wow-moment line, an optional preview still, and the two unmissable outward links (**Live demo** → `demoUrl`, **GitHub** → `repoUrl`). Each bay carries its signature accent hue. The bay-to-bay transition is the threshold/shaft-of-light motif.
4. **The directory (arrival)** — a calm, fully-legible index of all six projects as a grid/list (name, one-line pitch, category badge, both links), plus the **portfolio range statement** (sourced from the root README "Philosophy" + PROGRESS composition tracker: "six showcases, four api-heavy across four distinct backends — Elysia/Bun, Hono, NestJS, Fastify — plus two creative web-only showcases; one quality bar across all"). This is the no-cinema floor: everything reachable immediately, every link present. It doubles as the no-JS render.
5. **About / author + contact** — a tight statement of who this is and the portfolio philosophy ("polish over breadth; variance is the point; quality is non-negotiable" — from the root README, in the author's voice), the author's name and contact (email; and the `GITHUB_BASE` profile link once real), and a final "explore" affordance.
6. **Footer** — strong brand presence: the `ATRIUM` wordmark, a compact repeat of the six project links, contact, social/profile icons (`lucide-react`), and a legal/credits line. A designed surface, not a sitemap dump.

A persistent, accessible header appears after the hero: brand mark (left), anchor nav to the six projects + the directory (center, collapses to a menu on mobile), theme toggle, and a quiet contact/"view all" affordance (right).

## Success criteria

All measurable; all gate v1 ship.

- **Lighthouse ≥ 95** in **all four** categories (Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, SEO) on the deployed page, measured via Lighthouse CI, median of 5 runs. This page is the portfolio's front door — its scores are judged hardest and must clear the bar with margin, with no scoped exception (unlike tape's documented live-route carve-out).
- **Core Web Vitals green:** LCP < 2.5 s (the LCP element is the hero wordmark or its lit backdrop — type/CSS-light, so this should be comfortably green; any preview image is lazy and never the LCP), CLS < 0.1 (the pinned descent + bays must pin via transform and reserve layout — zero shift), INP < 200 ms (scroll is rAF-driven via GSAP/ScrollTrigger; no long tasks during the scrub; the six-bay sequence must not jank on a mid-tier laptop).
- **Wow moment quality bar.** The hero descent and the six-bay sequence run at a smooth 60 fps on a mid-tier laptop (no dropped frames during the pinned scrub, verified in a DevTools performance trace) and degrade correctly under `prefers-reduced-motion` (static directory page, no pin/scrub) and no-JS (full directory in real DOM). The designer-critic signs off against Olivier Larose + Stripe with zero pochwał **and** explicitly confirms it does not read as a razors-edge re-skin.
- **Every project is reachable in two clicks or fewer, and every link works.** All six demo links resolve to the live Fly URLs; all six repo links resolve from `GITHUB_BASE` (placeholder until a remote exists — and that fact is invisible/graceful to the viewer, never a broken-looking link). The directory and footer make all twelve outward links reachable without scrolling through the cinema.
- **Responsive from 320 px upward.** The hero, the six bays, the directory, and the footer are usable and on-brand at 320 px, tested up through 430 px mobile, tablet, and desktop. The pinned bay choreography reduces/simplifies on small widths (a `matchMedia` branch) so it stays legible and cheap on phones; the directory is the canonical mobile-first reachable index.
- **Theming.** Light + dark via CSS variables + `next-themes`, dark default, `prefers-color-scheme` respected, no FOUC on theme. The light "architectural daylight" theme is reviewed in its own right; the six signature hues are contrast-verified in both themes.
- **Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA.** Full keyboard navigation across the header nav, all six bays' links, the directory, and the footer (every outward link is a real, focusable `<a>` with a discernible name, e.g. "tape — live demo", "tape — GitHub repo"). Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for text in both themes, including each signature hue used as text/accent. Visible brand focus states, never removed. **`prefers-reduced-motion` MUST fully neutralise the scroll choreography to a static, readable page** — no pin, no scrub, no descent, no parallax; the page becomes the directory rendered top-to-bottom, every project and link present and legible, nothing frozen mid-transition. The wordmark text and every pitch/link are real server-rendered DOM regardless of the animation, so screen readers and crawlers always get the full content.
- **SEO — this is the portfolio's front page, so SEO matters most here.** Per-page meta + Open Graph + Twitter card, a **designed OG image** (the atrium wordmark-in-light composition), `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, and **JSON-LD**: a `Person` (the author) and a `WebSite`/`CollectionPage` (or an `ItemList` of the six projects as `CreativeWork`/`SoftwareApplication` entries with their demo URLs) so the front page is richly structured. Canonical URL via `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` (the documented placeholder pattern razors-edge/atlas use). Because this is intended as the eventual portfolio root (see AGENT_NOTES), the metadata is authored as the portfolio's primary metadata, not a sub-project's.
- **Security baseline.** Strict CSP headers (the GSAP-compatible posture razors-edge proved: `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'` for Next's inline bootstrap, **no `unsafe-eval'`** — GSAP core + ScrollTrigger are clean; `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` accepted for GSAP inline transforms + Tailwind), plus the full header set (`Strict-Transport-Security`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options`). All outward links carry `rel="noopener noreferrer"` and `target="_blank"`. No secrets; `.env.example` committed if any env is introduced (`NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_GITHUB_BASE`). No PII, no forms collecting data (contact is a `mailto:`/link, not a form, in v1).
- **Tests.** Vitest unit coverage on the `Project` Zod schema parse + the `projects.ts` config (all six present, in order, every `demoUrl` a valid URL, every `repoUrl` derives from `GITHUB_BASE`, categories/backends match the portfolio truth). Playwright E2E on: every project's demo + repo link is present and points where expected; the `prefers-reduced-motion` path renders the static directory (no pin); theme toggle in both themes; keyboard reachability of all twelve outward links. Lighthouse CI ≥ 95 ×4 in the pipeline.

## Out of scope (v1)

Locked. v2 candidates only.

- **Any real backend, database, or persistence.** atrium is a static typed index of six projects (the whole web-only thesis — DECISIONS.md ADR-001).
- **A CMS / admin to edit projects.** The six entries are a typed `src/data/projects.ts` module, not editable content. Adding a seventh project is a one-line array edit.
- **Restructuring the monorepo so atrium physically lives at the portfolio root.** v1 ships as a self-contained `projects/atrium/` Next app consistent with every sibling. The "this is the eventual root landing page" intent is recorded in AGENT_NOTES for a future hosting/routing decision; v1 does not attempt it.
- **Real GitHub links.** The repo has no remote (CLAUDE.md § 10). `repoUrl` derives from a single `GITHUB_BASE` placeholder; flipping it to real is a one-constant change recorded for the moment a remote exists. No broken inline `github.com/...` links are shipped.
- **Surfacing the internal "Fly demos stopped to control cost" status anywhere public.** Demo links are normal/live by deliberate owner policy (the demos auto-start on URL hit).
- **WebGL / R3F / 3D.** The atrium-of-light is CSS/SVG + parallax planes; § 15 forbids R3F for "fancy 2D backgrounds" and the performance budget on the most-judged page forbids the GPU-3D cost.
- **A contact form / newsletter / analytics-heavy instrumentation.** Contact is a `mailto:`/profile link in v1; no form collects data, no PII.
- **Per-project deep micro-sites inside atrium.** atrium links _out_ to each project's own deployed demo and repo; it does not re-host or embed them.
- **Internationalisation / multi-language.** English only (CLAUDE.md § 2 file-language rule).
- **Club-GreenSock-only GSAP plugins** (SplitText, MorphSVG). v1 assumes free GSAP core + ScrollTrigger only; a Club plugin requires its own ADR (the kinetic title resolves use real-DOM type + CSS + GSAP transforms, the razors-edge no-Club-plugin technique).
