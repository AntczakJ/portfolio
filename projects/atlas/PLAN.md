# atlas — PLAN

> Live geospatial fleet / delivery tracking. A server-side, deterministic simulation moves a fleet of vehicles along real routes in real time; their telemetry streams over WebSocket to a live map that feels alive — markers glide smoothly between server ticks (client-side interpolation), route trails draw, ETAs tick down, and geofence enter/exit events fire live.
> Brand display: **Atlas**. Repo dir: `atlas`. Portfolio slot 6, api-heavy, **Fastify** (Node 22 LTS).

## Problem

Recruiters and engineers evaluating a fullstack portfolio have seen plenty of dashboards and CRUD apps. What is rare — and what reads instantly as "this person can build real-time, spatial, systems-shaped software" — is a live map that genuinely feels alive: a fleet of vehicles moving in real time, smoothly, along real roads, with live ETAs, route progress, and geofence events firing as vehicles cross boundaries. The hard parts a real fleet/delivery product (Onfleet, Samsara, Bringg, Wise Systems, Uber/Lyft ops consoles) is built around are exactly the parts that do not survive a Next-only model: a long-lived high-frequency telemetry stream, a background simulation engine that advances state on a tick, and server-side geospatial domain logic (route progress along a polyline, ETA computation, geofence enter/exit detection) worth isolating from the UI and testing in isolation. Atlas is that system, built small but real — the simulation is a genuine server-side engine, deterministic and replayable, and the map reacts to it in real time over a genuine WebSocket, never a polling loop.

The credibility line: the simulation is **server-authoritative** (positions, headings, speeds, ETAs, route progress, and geofence transitions are all computed server-side from a seeded engine), and the smoothness on screen is **client-side interpolation between authoritative ticks**, not faked motion. The map is the centerpiece and is judged on one question: "does it feel alive and real?"

## Audience

Two concrete viewers, one design that serves both.

1. **Senior fullstack / backend recruiter or engineer (US / EU), the primary.** Arrives at the demo URL from a CV link or a share. Lands on a 1440 px+ desktop monitor, frequently opens DevTools out of habit — to confirm the live channel is a real WebSocket carrying telemetry frames, not a `setInterval` polling JSON. They must read "this person can build real-time spatial systems — a simulation engine, a telemetry stream, server-side geo domain logic" within the **first 10 seconds**, before reading a word of the README. The wow moment delivers that proof visually: a map that is visibly, smoothly alive, with markers gliding along roads and events firing live.
2. **An operations-minded evaluator (dispatcher / logistics PM), the secondary.** Reads Atlas as a product: can I see the whole fleet at a glance, click a vehicle to see its route / ETA / status, watch a delivery progress, and understand geofence (depot / zone) events? This viewer exercises the fleet list, the vehicle detail / focus flow, the ETA panel, and the events feed. Mobile (down to 320 px) matters because a dispatcher glances at a fleet on a phone — the map must be usable, or degrade to a legible list/table view, on a small screen.

The design optimises for viewer 1's 10-second judgement (the live map is the lead surface) while making viewer 2's flows genuinely complete and genuinely usable, and while meeting the hard accessibility bar for a map-centric product (a keyboard-navigable, screen-reader-legible **non-map alternative**, see Accessibility).

## Wow moment

**A live operations map where the fleet moves smoothly in real time and the world reacts as it moves.**

Concretely, what holds the viewer for five seconds, on the main map surface:

- The map renders a fleet of vehicle markers, each with a heading (the marker rotates to face its direction of travel), a status color, and a label. On first paint it is already populated and already moving — not a static screenshot, not a "press play" empty state.
- The vehicles **glide** along their routes. The server emits an authoritative telemetry tick at a fixed cadence (e.g. every 1 s); the client **interpolates between ticks with `requestAnimationFrame`**, so a marker moving at, say, 12 m/s does not jump 12 m every second — it slides continuously along the road geometry between its last known position and its next, at a smooth 60 fps. A skeptic who opens DevTools sees a single WebSocket frame per second per the protocol, yet the screen is buttery — that gap between "1 Hz data" and "60 fps motion" is the senior signal made visible.
- **Route trails** draw behind each vehicle (a fading polyline of where it has been) and the **remaining route** is drawn ahead (the planned path to its destination), so the viewer reads intent, not just a moving dot.
- **Live ETAs tick down.** Selecting a vehicle (or watching the fleet panel) shows a live ETA to its next stop / destination that decrements in real time and recomputes as the vehicle progresses — derived server-side from remaining route distance and current speed, not a static label.
- **Geofence events fire live.** Depots and delivery zones are drawn as polygons on the map. As a vehicle crosses a boundary, a geofence **enter** or **exit** event fires live: the zone briefly highlights, an event row materialises at the top of the events feed ("Truck 7 entered Downtown Zone"), and the vehicle's status may flip (e.g. "en route" → "at stop"). The dramatic beat: watching a delivery vehicle approach a zone, cross the boundary, the zone pulse, and the event land — all in real time, all driven by the real engine.
- A **"focus a vehicle"** affordance (click a marker or a fleet-list row) smoothly flies the camera to the vehicle and pins the detail panel (route, stops, ETA, speed, recent events), so a recruiter who will not wait can immediately drive the experience and see the depth.

This is the real-time-spatial-systems story made visual: a recruiter does not take the simulation / stream / geo-logic story on faith from the README — they watch the fleet move, smoothly, and the world react.

**Reference alignment (`docs/inspirations.md`).** The map surface and its motion register are judged against **Linear** (the "this feels alive" micro-interaction bar and easing — the marker glide, the camera fly-to, and the event-row enter take their easing from Linear's `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`-class curves, not a generic `linear` ramp) and **Vercel** (dark, dense, deliberate — an operations/control-room surface that shows a lot of live data without feeling busy). The premium finish of the panels and the events feed is judged against **Stripe** (production-grade, "the company spent real money on this"). The designer-critic gates every UI milestone against at least two of these by name, zero pochwał.

### Wow fallback (mandatory — defined now, not deferred)

The wow depends on WebGL (MapLibre GL renders on a WebGL canvas) and on JavaScript / a live socket. A robust degradation story is a hard requirement, not an afterthought:

- **No WebGL / WebGL context lost.** If the browser reports no WebGL support (or a `webglcontextlost` event fires and cannot be restored), the map surface degrades to the **fleet table/list view** — the same authoritative telemetry rendered as a live, sortable table (vehicle, status, current zone, speed, ETA, % route complete) that updates from the same WebSocket. The product stays fully functional; only the spatial canvas is gone. A non-blocking notice explains the fallback.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`.** The marker interpolation tween, the camera fly-to, the route-trail draw, the zone pulse, and the event-row enter all collapse: markers **snap to each authoritative tick position** (no rAF tween), the camera **cuts** instead of flying, trails render statically, and event rows appear without motion. The map still updates live; it simply does not animate between states. The fleet stays fully legible.
- **No JavaScript (SSR floor).** The marketing/landing route and the public read surfaces render meaningful SSR content with no JS: the product pitch, a static snapshot of the fleet (a server-rendered table of the current seeded fleet state and the latest known positions), the route/zone reference, and the full SEO surface. The live map and live updates require JS (the WebSocket does), and that is honest — but the page is never blank without JS, and the SEO-bearing surfaces score Lighthouse ≥ 95 from SSR/static content.
- **Socket down / reconnect.** If the WebSocket drops, the client shows a discreet "reconnecting" indicator, freezes markers at their last authoritative position (no dead-reckoning drift past a small cap), and on reconnect requests a fresh snapshot frame and reconciles — never silently shows stale motion as if live.

## Stack flavour

**`api-heavy`.** This is the deliberate purpose of slot 6, and it meets the `docs/conventions.md` § 10 triggers decisively — **three of the five fire**, any one of which is sufficient:

- **WebSocket trigger.** The fleet telemetry is pushed over a long-lived WebSocket at high frequency (a tick per second per vehicle, plus event frames). A Next route handler cannot own a persistent, high-frequency fan-out channel tied to a continuously-running engine; this is the textbook long-lived-bidirectional case.
- **Background jobs / simulation trigger.** The simulation engine is a continuously-running server-side loop that advances N vehicles along routes on a fixed tick, independent of any HTTP request — a stateful background process, not a request-response handler. It runs whether or not a browser is connected.
- **Heavy domain logic trigger.** Route progress along a polyline (projecting a point onto a multi-segment LineString and advancing distance-along-route), ETA computation from remaining distance and speed, and geofence enter/exit detection (point-in-polygon transition tracking with debounce) are genuine geospatial domain logic that benefits from being unit-tested in isolation from the UI — exactly the "test in isolation" trigger.

`web-only` is rejected outright: the simulation engine alone forecloses it, and the WebSocket and the geo domain logic reinforce the call. There is no honest way to build a server-authoritative live-fleet product inside Next route handlers.

## Backend choice — Fastify (Node 22 LTS)

**Selected: Fastify (Node 22 LTS) + `@fastify/websocket` + Drizzle ORM + PostgreSQL + Zod (via `fastify-type-provider-zod`).**

Rationale serves the two masters from `docs/conventions.md` § 11:

(a) **Fits this project.** Atlas's server is a focused, performance-sensitive surface: a WebSocket endpoint fanning high-frequency telemetry to many clients, a continuously-running simulation loop, a small set of REST/read endpoints (fleet snapshot, routes, zones, vehicle detail, public read), and the geo domain logic. Fastify is the mature, fast, plugin-first Node server that fits this exactly: `@fastify/websocket` is a first-class, well-maintained WS plugin built on `ws`; Fastify's schema-first design pairs cleanly with shared Zod schemas via `fastify-type-provider-zod` (the same `src/lib/schemas/` contract the Next client imports); its plugin/encapsulation model keeps the simulation engine, the WS gateway, the geo services, and the REST routes as clean, separately-testable units without the decorator/DI ceremony NestJS would impose on a surface this size. The request-path performance and low overhead matter because the WS fan-out and the per-tick broadcast are the hot path. NestJS would be over-structured for this surface; Hono and Elysia are already taken (see below); Fastify is the precise fit — and notably the §11 backend the portfolio has not yet exercised.

(b) **Advances portfolio backend variance — and completes it.** Per the root `PROGRESS.md` composition tracker: tape (slot 1) = **Elysia on Bun** (thin-and-fast, Bun-native), meld (slot 2) = **Hono on Node** (multi-runtime, edge-ready), pulse (slot 4) = **NestJS on Node** (opinionated, modular, enterprise). Fastify is the **one § 11 backend not yet used**. Atlas commits **Fastify on Node**, taking the portfolio to **four api-heavy projects across four distinct backends** — every framework in § 11 now represented. After Atlas, the backend-variance story (§ 12) is not merely satisfied with margin, it is **complete**: thin-and-fast Elysia/Bun · multi-runtime-edge Hono · opinionated-enterprise NestJS · mature-fast-plugin Fastify. This is the load-bearing portfolio reason for the choice and is recorded in the tracker.

(c) **Runtime: Node 22 LTS.** Fastify, `@fastify/websocket` (`ws`), Drizzle (`postgres-js`), and the geo libraries are all best-paved on Node. No Bun deviation — the engineering budget goes into the simulation engine, the telemetry stream, and the smooth-interpolation client, not runtime-compat debugging. Pin in `.nvmrc`.

The architect ratifies the deeper designs (the WS frame contract, the simulation-engine tick model and determinism, the geo-math placement and the persistence posture, and the deploy topology) in follow-up ADRs at the start of implement — see "Deferred to architect".

## Map engine + tile source — MapLibre GL JS, keyless by default (HARD CONSTRAINT, FLAGGED)

**Selected: MapLibre GL JS (open-source, no paid SDK key) for the map. Tile source must render with NO paid secret committed to the repo.**

This is a **hard constraint flagged for the architect and every engineer**: the committed repo must render the map with no paid/secret key. The planner's recommendation, to be ratified by the architect in an ADR:

- **Primary recommendation — a free/keyless raster or vector basemap.** Concretely: a keyless OSM raster tile source is the zero-dependency floor, but raster tiles cannot rotate-with-bearing cleanly and look less "control-room". The preferred path is a **free vector basemap** rendered by MapLibre, with the **tile/style key treated as an OPTIONAL env var**: if `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_TILE_KEY` (or equivalent) is present (e.g. a MapTiler / Stadia / Protomaps free-tier key the owner supplies at deploy time), Atlas uses the richer vector style; if it is absent, Atlas falls back to a **keyless source** (a keyless raster style, or self-hosted/Protomaps-style demo tiles) so the repo and a fresh checkout render with zero secrets. The map MUST be visually acceptable in the keyless fallback — the architect prototypes both and pins the exact source.
- **`.env.example` documents the optional key** (clearly marked optional, with the keyless fallback behaviour described). `.env` is never committed. No paid secret is ever in the committed repo — verified.
- **Self-hosting note (architect to weigh).** For maximum control and a guaranteed-keyless premium look, self-hosting a small vector tile extract (Protomaps `.pmtiles` of the demo city, served as a static asset or via a tiny range-request handler) is the strongest keyless option and removes any third-party tile dependency at demo time. The architect weighs this (best look, fully keyless, more setup) against the free-tier-key path (simpler, depends on a free key the owner supplies) in the map ADR. Either way the keyless fallback is mandatory.

Why MapLibre and not alternatives: Mapbox GL JS requires a paid access token and a non-open license (rejected — violates the keyless constraint). Leaflet is raster-DOM-tile based, cannot do the smooth WebGL marker motion / rotation / camera fly-to at the quality bar, and would make the "feels alive" wow harder (rejected as the primary; the keyless raster fallback inside MapLibre covers the no-vector case). MapLibre GL JS is the open-source fork of Mapbox GL JS, WebGL-rendered, keyless-capable, supports vector styles, marker rotation, custom layers, and smooth camera animation — exactly what the wow needs. The architect ratifies the marker-rendering approach (HTML markers vs a GeoJSON symbol layer vs a custom WebGL layer) against the smooth-interpolation requirement.

## Real-time transport — Fastify WebSocket (`@fastify/websocket`)

**Selected: WebSocket over a single long-lived connection via `@fastify/websocket`. SSE is rejected for this project (deliberately — and articulating why is part of the signal).**

The portfolio already ships an SSE project (pulse) and the planner there made the textbook "one-directional fan-out → SSE" argument. Atlas is the deliberate WebSocket counterpart, and the choice is justified, not defaulted:

- **High-frequency binary-friendly telemetry.** The fleet telemetry is a per-tick broadcast of N vehicle positions; at portfolio scale this is small but it is the kind of continuous high-rate stream where WebSocket's lighter per-message framing (vs SSE's text/event-stream line protocol) and its support for binary frames (if the architect chooses a compact binary tick encoding) is the right tool. WebSocket is the production-faithful transport for live-fleet telemetry — what the real products use.
- **Genuinely bidirectional.** Unlike pulse (where every client action was an ordinary HTTP request), Atlas has a real client→server channel over the same socket: subscribe/unsubscribe to a viewport or a focused vehicle (so the server can send only the relevant subset), request a fresh snapshot on reconnect, and (demo) control the simulation (pause / resume / set speed / inject an event). This bidirectional control over one socket is exactly what WebSocket is for and what SSE cannot do — the honest distinguishing reason.
- **The DevTools tell.** Viewer 1 opening the Network tab sees one WebSocket connection with telemetry frames streaming — unambiguous proof the map is genuinely pushed. Combined with the 1 Hz-data / 60 fps-motion gap (interpolation), this is the senior signal.
- **Contrast with pulse is itself a portfolio signal.** Atlas (WebSocket, bidirectional, high-frequency spatial) and pulse (SSE, one-directional, status fan-out) demonstrate that the portfolio picks the real-time transport per the problem, not by habit. The architect pins the frame contract, the snapshot-vs-delta model, heartbeat/reconnect, and backpressure in the WS-contract ADR.

The architect ratifies the WS frame contract (snapshot frame, delta/tick frame, event frame, control frames), the encoding (JSON vs a compact binary/MessagePack tick), per-connection subscription scoping, heartbeat keep-alive under the Fly edge idle timeout, and reconnect/snapshot-resume semantics in the real-time-contract ADR.

## Simulation engine — server-side, deterministic, replayable

**Selected: an in-process, server-authoritative simulation loop in the Fastify server (worker/loop), deterministic from a seed, advancing N vehicles along predefined routes on a fixed tick.** The architect ratifies the tick model, the determinism strategy, and whether it runs in the main process or a split worker.

Design the planner commits (architect deepens in ADR):

- **Routes are predefined polylines (GeoJSON LineString)** for a single demo city, each with ordered **stops** (delivery/pickup points) and a total length. A route is the geometry a vehicle follows; the demo ships a hand-curated set of plausible city routes (see Simulation / mock data shape).
- **A vehicle has a position-along-route** (a scalar distance `s` in metres from the route start) plus a speed (m/s, varied per vehicle and modulated by simple rules — slow near stops, dwell at stops, resume). Each tick advances `s` by `speed * dt`; the engine **projects `s` back to a lat/lng + heading** by walking the route polyline (the geo domain logic). At a stop, the vehicle dwells for a configured duration (status "at stop"), then continues; at route end it either loops, reverses, or is assigned a new route (architect picks the demo behaviour).
- **The tick emits authoritative telemetry:** per vehicle `{ id, lat, lng, heading, speed, routeId, distanceAlongRoute, progressPct, status, nextStopId, etaSeconds }`. ETA = remaining route distance to the next stop / current (or rolling-average) speed. This is the frame the WS broadcasts and the client interpolates between.
- **Geofence detection runs each tick:** for each vehicle, test its position against the zone polygons; a transition (was-outside → now-inside, or the reverse), **debounced** to avoid boundary chatter, emits a `geofence.enter` / `geofence.exit` event with `{ vehicleId, zoneId, at }`. The vehicle's status may update.
- **Determinism + replay.** The engine is seeded (`faker.seed(n)` for the fleet/route assignment; a fixed tick `dt`; a deterministic speed-modulation function), so a given seed + elapsed-tick-count produces an exactly reproducible world. This makes the demo reproducible, the tests deterministic, and a **replay** mode possible (rewind/scrub to tick T). The wow-moment beats (a specific vehicle entering a specific zone) are reproducible on demand because the engine is deterministic — the demo can seek to the tick just before the event for a recruiter. The architect pins the determinism contract (pure tick function `tick(state, dt) -> { state, events }`, no wall-clock inside the reducer, time supplied) so the engine is a pure, unit-testable core with an IO shell around it.
- **Where it runs.** Recommendation: the engine is a pure tick reducer; an IO shell (a `setInterval`-class loop, or a worker process) drives it, applies each tick, and hands telemetry + events to the WS gateway and the persistence/event sink. The architect decides single-process vs split worker (mirroring pulse's web/worker question) against the deploy budget — the pure-reducer boundary means the topology choice does not touch the domain logic.

## Geo math placement — turf.js (shared FE + BE), PostGIS deferred

**Recommendation (architect ratifies): `@turf/turf` (or scoped `@turf/*` modules) for the geo math, used on both server and client, sharing the algorithms through `src/lib/geo/`; PostgreSQL WITHOUT PostGIS for v1 persistence.**

- **turf.js** provides the exact primitives the engine and the client need: `along` (point at distance `s` along a LineString), `nearestPointOnLine` / `pointToLineDistance` (projection for progress), `length` (route distance), `bearing` (heading), `booleanPointInPolygon` (geofence test), `distance`. Using the same library on server (authoritative computation) and client (e.g. drawing the remaining-route slice, interpolation geometry) keeps the math consistent and the algorithms shareable through `src/lib/geo/` — the same single-source-of-truth discipline conventions § 5 applies to schemas. The geo functions are pure and unit-tested (the "heavy domain logic" trigger made concrete).
- **PostgreSQL without PostGIS for v1.** The persistence need is modest: store the route/zone/stop definitions (as GeoJSON in `jsonb`), the vehicle/fleet metadata, and a bounded telemetry/event history for the snapshot, the events feed, and seed-vs-live. The authoritative geo computation happens **in the engine (turf), not in the DB** — the simulation is the source of truth, not spatial SQL queries. PostGIS would add real geo-query power (and a genuine "real geo DB" wow), but it also adds a heavier Postgres image, extension setup, and deploy friction, for query power v1 does not exercise (we are not doing arbitrary spatial queries over a large dataset — we have a small, known fleet and a handful of zones computed in-engine). The architect weighs this explicitly: **recommended path is plain Postgres + `jsonb` GeoJSON + turf-in-engine** for deploy simplicity (mirroring how pulse/tape keep the DB lean on Fly), with **PostGIS noted as the v2 upgrade** if a real spatial-query feature (e.g. "vehicles within radius", server-side viewport culling via spatial index) is added. The architect may overturn this if a prototype shows PostGIS earns its keep — but the default leans lean, matching the portfolio's Fly deploy posture.

## Animation stack — the map is the wow; Motion for UI/panel transitions only

**The map and its motion are the wow, and are NOT driven by a general animation library.** Per `docs/conventions.md` § 15 (do not stack libraries), the project's single declarative animation library is **Motion**, scoped strictly to React-state UI/panel transitions. The load-bearing real-time motion lives outside it:

- **Marker interpolation = a hand-rolled `requestAnimationFrame` tween** between authoritative ticks (lerp position along the road geometry, slerp/shortest-arc the heading), driven by MapLibre's imperative API (updating a GeoJSON source or marker transforms off the React render path). This is NOT Motion — per-marker per-frame animation through Motion/React state would be a frame-budget disaster (the exact class of mistake pulse's "the streaming surface is not Motion" and tape's "Canvas2D is not Motion" rules guard against). The rAF tween owns the map repaint.
- **Camera fly-to** uses MapLibre's native `flyTo` / `easeTo` (its own animation), not Motion.
- **Route-trail draw, zone pulse** are MapLibre layer/paint updates (and CSS where DOM), not Motion.
- **Motion (single declarative library, § 15)** is scoped to the React UI chrome: the vehicle detail panel slide-in (`AnimatePresence`), the events-feed row enter/exit, the fleet-list selection state, toasts, the theme crossfade, dialog/sheet transitions. State-driven micro-interactions — Motion's default register. GSAP and R3F are rejected: no scroll timeline, no SVG-path choreography, no general 3D (MapLibre owns the WebGL canvas; we are not adding R3F on top).

The frontend-engineer respects this split. Adding a second declarative animation library requires an ADR.

## Success criteria

All measurable, all gate v1 ship:

- **The map feels alive and real.** On first paint the fleet is already moving; markers glide at ~60 fps via client-side rAF interpolation between authoritative server ticks (verified: with the server ticking at 1 Hz, marker motion is continuous, not stepped). DevTools shows exactly one WebSocket connection carrying telemetry frames — no polling XHR loop. This is the wow and the lead success criterion.
- **The simulation is server-authoritative and deterministic.** Positions, headings, speeds, ETAs, route progress, and geofence transitions are computed server-side from a seeded engine; the same seed + tick count reproduces the same world (unit-tested). The smoothness is interpolation, not faked client motion — documented honestly.
- **Geofence events fire live and correctly.** A vehicle crossing a zone boundary fires exactly one `enter` (and later one `exit`) event — debounced so boundary chatter does not spawn duplicates (unit-tested: a vehicle skimming a boundary does not flap). The event lands on the events feed and (where applicable) flips the vehicle status, live, within ~1 tick of the crossing.
- **ETAs are live and correct.** A focused vehicle's ETA to its next stop decrements in real time and recomputes from remaining route distance and speed (unit-tested against fixtures with known route geometry and speed → known ETA, within tolerance).
- **The wow beats are reproducible on demand.** Because the engine is deterministic and seekable, a demo affordance (focus a vehicle approaching a zone; or a "replay this delivery" / scrub control) reliably reproduces the cross-the-boundary-and-fire-the-event beat for a recruiter, every time, on the deployed demo.
- **Keyless map.** The committed repo renders the map with NO paid secret. A fresh checkout (no `.env`) renders the keyless fallback basemap and the fleet on it. The optional tile key is documented in `.env.example` and only enriches the basemap when supplied. Verified — no secret committed.
- **Graceful degradation holds.** No-WebGL → live fleet table view (same data, same socket). `prefers-reduced-motion` → markers snap to ticks, camera cuts, no tweens, still live. No-JS → SSR floor (pitch + static fleet snapshot table + SEO), never blank. Socket drop → reconnect indicator + freeze + snapshot reconcile, never stale-as-live. All four verified.
- **Lighthouse ≥ 95 in all four categories** on the SEO-bearing surface (the landing / public read page), on the deployed URL. Meta tags, OG image (`next/og`), JSON-LD, `sitemap`, `robots`. SSR/static-friendly first paint with no client-only data dependency. The live map dashboard is auth-or-not but is documented exempt from the Lighthouse-SEO target (client-only live-data first paint, no crawler value) — but it must still feel fast and not jank.
- **Security.** CSP headers strict and **no `unsafe-eval`** — and crucially the CSP must explicitly allow what MapLibre needs (a Web **Worker** for tile parsing → `worker-src 'self' blob:`; `blob:` for the worker; the tile host in `connect-src` and `img-src` for raster; `data:`/`blob:` as needed) WITHOUT opening `unsafe-eval`; verify under a prod build, not `next dev` (the meld/razors-edge/pulse lesson). Rate limiting on the public WS (connection rate + per-connection message rate) and on the public REST endpoints. All input (WS control frames, REST) validated by shared Zod schemas. `.env.example` committed, never `.env`. The simulation has no user-supplied URL/SSRF surface (unlike pulse) — but the WS control channel is an input surface and is Zod-validated and rate-limited.
- **Theming.** Light + dark via CSS variables + `next-themes`, respecting `prefers-color-scheme`. **The map style must have a matching dark + light basemap** (a dark control-room style and a clean light style) — the theme toggle switches the MapLibre style, not just the chrome. **Sovereign design tokens** — Atlas is a fresh operations/control-room identity; NO token reuse from tape / meld / razors-edge / pulse / apex (`docs/conventions.md` § 14). Both themes intentional and reviewed in their own right.
- **Responsive.** Renders correctly from 320 px upward, mobile-first. The map is usable on a phone (touch pan/zoom, a bottom-sheet vehicle detail), or degrades to the legible fleet list/table on the narrowest viewport. The panels reflow; the events feed collapses to a tab/sheet on narrow screens.
- **Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA, with the map-specific story made explicit.** A WebGL map is not natively accessible, so Atlas ships a genuine **non-map alternative**: a keyboard-navigable, screen-reader-legible **fleet table** (the same data as the map) that is a first-class view, not a hidden fallback — togglable by any user, and the default for `no-WebGL`. Map markers have accessible names; vehicle status is never color-alone (status carries a text label / icon); the focus flow is keyboard-operable (tab through the fleet list, Enter to focus a vehicle); live event announcements go to an `aria-live="polite"` region without flooding. Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for all chrome. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected (see fallback). Map pan/zoom has keyboard equivalents (MapLibre supports keyboard nav) and the table view is the full keyboard/SR path.

## Simulation / mock data shape

Ratified/refined by the architect in ADR-002 + ADR-005. Indicative, not final.

- **One demo city** (a real city's road geometry, or a plausible hand-authored grid) so the routes look real and the zones make sense. Keyless tiles must cover it.
- **routes** — `id`, `name`, `geometry` (GeoJSON LineString in `jsonb`), `length_m` (precomputed via turf), an ordered set of stops. ~6–12 curated routes that look like delivery/service routes across the city.
- **route_stops** — `id`, `route_id`, `seq`, `name`, `point` (GeoJSON Point), `dwell_seconds`. Pickups/deliveries/depots along a route.
- **vehicles** — `id`, `label` (e.g. "Truck 7"), `type` (van/truck/courier), `route_id`, `distance_along_route_m` (live, in-engine), `speed_mps`, `status` (`en_route` | `at_stop` | `idle` | `returning`), `current_zone_id` (nullable). The fleet: ~12–30 vehicles for a lively-but-legible map.
- **zones / geofences** — `id`, `name`, `kind` (`depot` | `delivery_zone` | `restricted`), `geometry` (GeoJSON Polygon in `jsonb`). ~4–8 zones (a couple of depots + several delivery zones).
- **events** — `id`, `vehicle_id`, `zone_id` (nullable), `type` (`geofence.enter` | `geofence.exit` | `status.change` | `arrived` | `departed`), `at`. The events feed + bounded history.
- **telemetry snapshot** — the latest authoritative per-vehicle telemetry for the snapshot frame + the SSR floor + reconnect reconcile (the engine is the live source; this is a periodically-persisted snapshot, not the per-tick firehose).
- **Determinism:** `faker.seed(n)` for fleet/route assignment + a fixed tick `dt` + a deterministic speed-modulation fn → a reproducible world; the demo can seek to a tick. Seed runs once, idempotent; live ticks run forward from the baseline. The "server-authoritative sim, client-interpolated motion, seed-vs-live boundary" is documented as the credibility line (mirrors pulse's seed-vs-live discipline).

## Out of scope (v1)

Locked. v2 candidates only.

- **Real GPS / real vehicles / real telematics ingestion.** v1 is a simulation. The architecture (engine → WS → map) would accept a real telemetry ingestor in place of the engine, noted as the v2 extension — but v1 does not ingest real devices.
- **Routing / pathfinding / road-snapping against a routing engine** (OSRM / Valhalla / GraphHopper). Routes are predefined polylines. Real turn-by-turn routing and road-snapping are v2.
- **PostGIS + arbitrary spatial queries** (vehicles-within-radius, spatial-index viewport culling). v1 computes geo in-engine (turf) over a small known fleet; PostGIS is the v2 upgrade if a real spatial-query feature is added.
- **Multi-tenant / dispatcher accounts / assignment workflows / driver app.** v1 is a single live operations view (read-mostly + demo control). No auth-gated dispatch, no driver-side, no order-assignment workflow.
- **Real ETA/traffic models.** ETA is remaining-distance / speed. Live traffic, historical-speed models, and time-of-day effects are v2.
- **Persisted long-horizon history / analytics / playback library.** v1 keeps a bounded telemetry/events window for the feed + snapshot + the deterministic replay; long-horizon trip history and analytics are v2.
- **Geofence rules engine** (configurable alerts, dwell-time alerts, speeding-in-zone). v1 fires enter/exit + status; a configurable rules engine is v2.
