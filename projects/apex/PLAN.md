# apex — PLAN

> Premium-modern / EV-flavoured marketing site for a car **rental** company, with an interactive 3D car configurator as the centerpiece wow and a fully mocked, delightful multi-step **rental reservation** flow.
> Brand display: **APEX**. Repo dir: `apex`.
> Structural foundation: this project is built on the **razors-edge** scaffold (same web-only Next 15 posture, same per-project file discipline, same sovereign-token + frozen-clock + shared-Zod-schema patterns). It deviates from razors-edge on exactly one load-bearing axis — it adds **React Three Fiber + drei** for a genuine 3D configurator — which is recorded as ADR-002 (see DECISIONS.md).

## Problem

Car rental companies — especially the premium / EV end of the market — sell access to a feeling: a clean, fast, quiet, technological car, available on your terms, picked up without friction. Their websites almost universally undersell that: a stock fleet grid of stretched press photos, a date-picker bolted to a third-party booking engine, fine print, and a "Reserve" button that hands you to a generic checkout with none of the brand's character. There is a gap between the premium-modern experience a good EV rental delivers and the flat, templated digital first impression it makes — and, specifically, the customer never gets to _see and shape the exact car_ before they reserve it. For this portfolio, `apex` closes that gap as a **web-only creative / marketing showcase**: a clean, technological, light-minimal site where the hero introduces the car on scroll, an **interactive 3D configurator** lets the visitor rotate it and swap colour and wheels live, a cinematic scroll-reveal gallery carries them through the fleet and the brand, and a fully mocked **rental reservation wizard** lets them choose a vehicle, a date range, pickup/return locations and extras, with a live price summary and a believable confirmation — all without a backend. It is the portfolio's proof that the author can carry a single arresting, _technological_ art direction across a complete marketing site, a real WebGL centrepiece, and a multi-step interactive flow, at production quality, without leaning on a server to look impressive.

## Audience

Dual audience, and the design must serve both in the same five seconds:

1. **The recruiter / peer judging craft (primary for portfolio value).** Lands on the deployed demo URL from a portfolio index, a shared link, or an Awwwards-style roundup. Desktop monitor (1440 px+ typical, 1920 px common), capable GPU, may open DevTools out of habit and _will_ check whether the "3D car" is a real WebGL scene or a sprite sheet. Decides in 5–10 seconds whether this is "another fleet template" or "someone who can art-direct, ship a real 3D configurator, and choreograph scroll without tanking performance". The configurator wow must feel live and tactile, and the reservation flow must feel like a real, considered product when they click through it. This audience is also the one most likely to load the site on a throttled connection or a mid-tier device to test the performance claims — so the WebGL must degrade honestly.
2. **The plausible real customer of a premium EV rental (primary for the fiction's credibility).** 28–50, booking a car for a weekend, a business trip, or to try an EV before buying one. Arrives on **mobile** as often as desktop (rental traffic skews mobile — a Maps listing, an Instagram bio link, a "rent an EV for the weekend" search). Wants: to see the actual cars, understand the daily price and what is included, pick up where they need to, add a child seat or extra insurance, and reserve a date range without friction — one-handed, at 320–430 px, possibly on cellular. This audience is why the reservation flow has to feel real even though nothing persists, why the configurator must have a graceful mobile fallback (a 3D scene that melts a mid-tier phone is a worse first impression than a crisp static render), and why mobile is a first-class target, not a desktop afterthought.

The art direction is tuned for audience 1; the information architecture, the reservation flow, and the mobile/no-WebGL fallbacks are tuned for audience 2. Neither is sacrificed for the other.

## Wow moment

The owner asked for three effects — a scroll-driven hero, a 3D configurator, and a cinematic scroll-reveal gallery. They are **reconciled into one coherent art-directed spine**, not three disjoint tricks. The spine is **"one car, brought closer":** the site is a single continuous push from _seeing_ the car, to _being able to touch and shape_ it, to _seeing it in the world_.

- **The centrepiece wow is the interactive 3D configurator.** A real React Three Fiber + drei scene of the hero EV that the visitor can **orbit 360°**, with **live colour swaps** (a small, art-directed paint palette) and **live wheel swaps** (2–3 wheel sets), reacting instantly — paint that actually re-shades the body, wheels that actually change geometry, lighting that reads as a studio shot. This is the thing that holds the recruiter for five seconds and the customer for thirty: a car they can spin and make _theirs_. The configured colour/wheel selection is the thread that ties the spine together — it **carries forward into the reservation flow** (the wizard's vehicle step reflects what you configured), so the configurator is not a toy off to the side; it is the entry point into the booking.
- **The scroll-hero leads _into_ the configurator.** On first paint the hero presents the car as a single arresting product shot (a `next/image` AVIF studio render — the LCP element — over a clean, light, technological field with the **APEX** wordmark and one line of positioning). As the visitor scrolls the first viewport, a GSAP `ScrollTrigger` timeline choreographs the introduction: the wordmark and UI recede, the car scales and settles toward its "hero pose", and the scene **hands off from the static hero render into the live WebGL configurator** — the moment the still image becomes a thing you can grab. (The hand-off is the design's single most important seam; see the technical contract deferred to ADR-004.) The hero scroll _is_ the transition into the centrepiece, so the scroll never stalls on a finished animation.
- **The cinematic gallery / scroll-reveal is the connective tissue.** Between the configurator and the rest of the site, and through the fleet and brand sections, a recurring scroll-reveal motif (clean, technological masked reveals — image and type sliding into place against the light field, parallaxed crops, a thin accent "track" line that recurs as the brand's signature) carries the eye. It is the same visual language as the hero and the configurator's studio lighting — the "track line" and the masked reveal are the recurring motifs that make the three effects read as one art direction. The gallery presents the wider fleet and the EV story (range, charging, the quiet-fast feeling) as a cinematic scroll, not a grid dump.

**Reference points** from `docs/inspirations.md`, with the specific sites the designer-critic judges against named: the configurator and the production-WebGL-in-a-marketing-page bar is judged against **Bruno Simon** (the canonical "what's possible with three.js in a portfolio" — the configurator must feel that alive and tactile) and **Stripe / Vercel ship pages** ("production-grade WebGL inside marketing pages" — WebGL that justifies its bundle cost, the model for _restraint_ so the 3D earns its weight rather than showing off); the scroll-hero and gallery scroll-reveal are judged against **Olivier Larose** (scroll-driven storytelling + photographic art direction) and **Stripe** (long-form scroll where a custom element justifies its bundle, type as a primary element); the chrome, type, and the technological-minimal restraint are judged against **Linear** and **Vercel** (restraint with energy, deliberate accents, dense information without busyness — the premium-modern register). The wordmark/type holds up as a specimen (**Klim** reference).

**A defined fallback is part of this spec, not an afterthought** (mirroring razors-edge's three-tier discipline, extended to cover WebGL):

- **Tier 1 — full (capable GPU, JS on, motion OK):** the live WebGL configurator (orbit + live colour/wheel swaps), the scroll-hero hand-off into it, and the full scroll-reveal gallery.
- **Tier 2 — `prefers-reduced-motion`:** no scrubbed scroll choreography and no auto-orbit. The configurator is still **interactive on direct user input** (the user can drag to orbit and tap colour/wheel swatches — that is user-initiated, not auto-motion), but the hero hand-off becomes a simple crossfade from the static render to the (still) configurator frame, the gallery reveals are static/instant, and any idle auto-rotate is disabled. If the architect determines (ADR-004) that even user-driven WebGL is undesirable under reduced-motion for some users, the documented fallback is Tier 3's static render with the swatches still re-rendering a _pre-baked_ image per colour/wheel — to be ratified in the ADR.
- **Tier 3 — no WebGL (failed/blocked WebGL context, very low-end GPU, or a `prefers-reduced-data`-style budget):** the configurator degrades to a **static, art-directed render gallery** — the same car shown as pre-rendered AVIF stills, and the colour/wheel swatches swap between **pre-baked images** (one render per colour × wheel combination, a small fixed matrix) instead of mutating a live scene. The visitor still gets to "configure" and see the result; it is just images, not a GPU scene. This is the floor the WebGL _enhances_; it is never the only thing that works.
- **Tier 4 — no-JS / failed hydration:** the hero renders as a static composed product shot with the real-DOM wordmark and positioning copy; the configurator section renders the default-configuration static render with real `alt` text; every section's content is real server-rendered DOM. WebGL and scroll only ever _enhance_ a page that already reads and is legible to crawlers and screen readers.

The wow is additive over a floor that always works. The configurator's pre-baked-render fallback (Tier 3) is a first-class deliverable, not a stub — it is also what makes the configurator usable on the mobile-customer's mid-tier phone where a live 60 fps WebGL scene is not guaranteed.

## Stack flavour

**`web-only`.**

This is an explicit, owner-confirmed decision, and it is correct against `docs/conventions.md` § 10. None of the five api-heavy triggers fires:

- **No WebSocket / SSE.** There is no long-lived bidirectional channel. The reservation flow is a client-side wizard over seeded mock data; nothing streams. The configurator runs entirely in the browser (WebGL on the client).
- **No background jobs / queue / cron.** There is no server-side scheduled work. Vehicle availability for a date range is computed deterministically from seeded mock data at render time on the client.
- **API not consumed by a non-Next client.** There is no public API. The only consumer of any data is this Next.js app itself.
- **No heavy auth flow.** There is no auth at all in v1. No accounts, no sessions, no tokens. The reservation flow collects contact/driver details into client state and shows a confirmation — it does not authenticate anyone.
- **No heavy domain logic worth isolating from the UI.** The "domain logic" here is (a) date-range availability + price computation and (b) the reservation-wizard + configurator state transitions. Both are small, pure, deterministic, and live perfectly well in `src/lib/` tested with Vitest — they do not benefit from a separate backend service. The reservation submission is mocked: a Next.js server action that validates with the shared Zod schema, waits a realistic beat, and returns a deterministic confirmation. That is the entire server surface.

**Note that the 3D configurator does _not_ make this api-heavy.** WebGL is a _client_ concern; the § 10 triggers are all _server_ concerns. A genuinely 3D project is a frontend-complexity signal (it changes the animation stack — see ADR-002), not a backend signal. `api-heavy` is rejected because forcing a backend here would be ceremony, not need — exactly the "deviate only when the project genuinely requires it" bar from CLAUDE.md § 3.

**Portfolio-composition note (planner enforcement, `docs/conventions.md` § 12).** This is the **second consecutive web-only project** (razors-edge slot 3 web-only, apex slot 4 web-only). The api-heavy slots are healthy: slot 1 `tape` (Elysia / Bun), slot 2 `meld` (Hono / Node), and slot 4… — wait: the _current_ composition (root `PROGRESS.md`) already has `pulse` occupying slot 4 as the api-heavy NestJS project, which means the portfolio is at **3 api-heavy projects across three distinct backends (Elysia/Bun, Hono/Node, NestJS) plus one web-only (razors-edge)** _before_ apex. apex therefore takes **slot 5** as a second web-only creative showcase. With 5 projects (3 api-heavy, 2 web-only) the § 12 ratio is **satisfied with margin** — the 2–3 api-heavy minimum and the ≥2-backends (here three) variance requirement are both met. **This is the owner's deliberate call and it does not breach § 12.** It is recorded so the next planning pass does not mistake a second consecutive web-only brief for drift — and it picks up the standing flag razors-edge's PLAN raised. See DECISIONS.md ADR-001 for the explicit composition flag and the recommendation that any _sixth_ project, if the portfolio grows, be weighed against keeping backend variance fresh (Fastify is the one § 11 backend not yet used). The api-heavy/backend-variance target is already met; this constraint does not block apex.

## Animation / 3D stack — R3F + drei (primary, the configurator) + GSAP + ScrollTrigger (scroll choreography)

**Selected: a deliberate, bounded two-library posture** per `docs/conventions.md` § 15 and CLAUDE.md § 5 (do **not** stack three):

1. **React Three Fiber + drei** — the genuine-3D primary, owning the configurator: the WebGL scene, the car model, orbit controls, live material (paint) and mesh (wheel) swaps, studio lighting/environment, and the camera. This is exactly the § 15 case for R3F: "only when the project actually uses WebGL / 3D" — the configurator _is_ real 3D, not a fancy 2D background. drei supplies the production helpers (`OrbitControls`/`PresentationControls`, `Environment`, `useGLTF` with draco/meshopt, `Bounds`, `ContactShadows`, `Html`, `useProgress`/`Loader`) that keep the scene production-grade and accessible.
2. **GSAP + ScrollTrigger** — the scroll-choreography primary, owning everything scroll-, pin-, scrub-, and timeline-driven: the scroll-hero introduction, the hand-off from the static hero render into the live configurator, the gallery scroll-reveal motif, the section "track line" reveals, and the reservation-section reveals. This is the § 15 case for GSAP: scroll-driven timelines + many independently animated elements where a declarative model fights you. razors-edge already proved the GSAP + Next 15 App Router integration pattern (`useGSAP()` + `gsap.matchMedia()` + transform/opacity-only + strict-CSP-clean), and apex inherits that pattern wholesale (see ADR-002 / ADR-004 follow-ups).

**Two libraries (R3F + GSAP) is the allowed, deliberate posture. Motion is explicitly NOT included by default.** Per the owner's brief and CLAUDE.md § 5, we do **not** add Motion as a third library. razors-edge used GSAP-primary + Motion-scoped-secondary (its wizard step transitions); apex's _two_ are R3F + GSAP, and the wizard step transitions here default to **GSAP timelines and/or CSS `data-state` transitions** rather than Motion. If, during the wizard build (Phase 5), small React-state component transitions prove genuinely awkward in CSS + GSAP, the frontend-engineer must **raise it in `AGENT_NOTES.md` for the architect to ratify or reject in an ADR** — Motion is admitted only by an explicit, bounded ADR decision, and the default is **not** to include it (single-purpose-per-library: R3F = 3D, GSAP = scroll + state-transition + SVG). This keeps apex honestly inside the "do not stack three" rule.

The R3F-vs-GSAP boundary is the same shape as razors-edge's: **R3F owns everything inside the WebGL canvas (the configurator scene, camera, materials, meshes, orbit). GSAP owns everything outside it that is scroll/pin/scrub/timeline-driven, and the DOM-side state transitions and SVG.** The one coupling point — the scroll-hero hand-off _into_ the canvas, where GSAP-driven scroll progress drives the camera/scene state at the seam — is the highest-risk integration and is pinned in ADR-004 (the hero/configurator hand-off technical contract). The architect ratifies the exact boundary and the GSAP↔R3F coupling mechanism in ADR-002.

**3D asset / model note (royalty-clear, with a swap-for-real path):** v1 uses a **royalty-clear placeholder GLTF/GLB car model** — candidate sources, in order of preference, are (a) a CC0 / royalty-clear model from Poly Haven, Sketchfab's CC0 filter, Khronos glTF Sample Models, or a similar clearly-licensed library, or (b) a clean primitive/stylised "concept EV" built or assembled so there is **no IP/trademark exposure** (no real manufacturer's badged car). The chosen model's provenance and license are documented in `AGENT_NOTES.md` and the README (mirroring razors-edge's photography `CREDITS.md` discipline), and the README states the **swap-for-real path** (drop in a licensed/commissioned brand model, re-export with draco/meshopt, point `useGLTF` at it). The model must be **draco- or meshopt-compressed** and budgeted (poly count + texture size) to fit the performance contract below.

GSAP licensing note: GSAP 3 core + ScrollTrigger are free under the standard no-charge license for this use; no Club-GreenSock-only plugins are assumed in v1 (same posture as razors-edge). R3F, drei, and three.js are MIT.

## Performance strategy for the WebGL configurator (load-bearing — protects Lighthouse ≥ 95)

The configurator is a real WebGL scene, and the quality bar (CLAUDE.md § 4) is Lighthouse ≥ 95 in all four categories _including on mid-tier mobile_. WebGL is the single biggest threat to that budget, so the strategy is part of the spec and is pinned by the architect in ADR-002 / ADR-004:

- **The hero LCP is never the WebGL canvas.** The hero's LCP element is the **static `next/image` AVIF product render**, `priority`, correctly sized, with a blur placeholder. The Canvas hydrates and the model loads _after_ first paint and _after_ the LCP, behind the static render. The hand-off (ADR-004) reveals the live canvas only once it is ready, so the WebGL boot cost never blocks LCP.
- **Lazy / dynamic import + Suspense.** The R3F `Canvas`, three.js, and the model are `next/dynamic`-imported with `ssr: false`, inside a Suspense boundary with a drei `<Loader>` / `useProgress` progress affordance, so three.js + the GLB are **not in the initial JS bundle** and do not count against the hero's first-load JS. The configurator code-splits into its own chunk loaded on approach (intersection / scroll proximity), not on page load.
- **Compressed, budgeted model.** The GLB is **draco or meshopt** compressed; a documented poly-count and texture-resolution budget keeps the asset small. Textures are KTX2/basis-compressed where it helps. The environment lighting uses a small/baked HDRI or drei's lightweight presets, not a heavy EXR.
- **Capped DPR + adaptive quality.** The `Canvas` uses a **capped `dpr`** (e.g. `[1, 2]` clamped, lower on mobile) and drei/R3F adaptive helpers (`AdaptiveDpr`, `AdaptiveEvents`, `PerformanceMonitor`) to drop resolution under load. The render loop is **on-demand** (`frameloop="demand"`) where possible — it renders on interaction/animation, not continuously, to spare battery and the main thread when idle.
- **Mobile + low-end fallback to the static/pre-baked path (Tier 3 above).** A capability/`prefers-reduced-data`/device-tier check (and a WebGL-context-failure catch) routes mid-tier mobile and no-WebGL clients to the **pre-baked-render configurator** (swatches swap AVIF stills) instead of the live scene. This is the same Tier-3 deliverable as the wow-moment fallback — it is what lets the project claim Lighthouse ≥ 95 on mobile honestly, because the heavy scene simply does not load there.
- **Measured, not asserted.** The Lighthouse ≥ 95 success criterion is verified on the **deployed** demo for both the hero route (where the canvas is deferred) and a configurator-focused measurement, and the mobile fallback path is the one Lighthouse-mobile measures. The architect fixes the exact device-tier heuristic and the on-demand/adaptive settings in ADR-002.

## Design direction

Sovereign visual identity — **no token reuse from tape, meld, razors-edge, or pulse** (`docs/conventions.md` § 14). Built from scratch in `app/globals.css`. This identity is the deliberate counterpoint to razors-edge's dark-luxe.

- **Aesthetic: premium-modern / EV — clean, technological, light minimalism with accents.** A bright, precise, _technological_ field (near-white / cool-light grounds, fine hairline structure, generous negative space, a confident grid), with one or two deliberate accents (a single saturated "EV" accent — an electric/voltaic hue — used with Linear/Vercel-style restraint, plus a near-black ink for type and structure). Premium and arresting through _precision and restraint_, explicitly **not** the dark-luxe atmosphere of razors-edge and explicitly **not** "clean and safe minimal" — the configurator, the scroll choreography, and the type are what make the restraint feel _designed_, not empty.
- **Light is the canonical theme and the default** (the planner's reasoned call; the architect may revisit in an ADR). Premium-modern / EV reads as a light register — bright studio light is the EV-product idiom (think clean spec-sheet pages and studio car photography), and a light canonical default is the deliberate contrast with razors-edge's dark-canonical identity, which strengthens the _range_ signal the portfolio exists to show. Per CLAUDE.md § 4 the site still ships **light + dark** via CSS variables + `next-themes`, with **light as default** and respecting `prefers-color-scheme`. The dark theme is **intentional, not an inversion**: a deep, cool "night drive / charging-bay" register — near-black cool grounds, the same EV accent shifted to glow on dark, the studio configurator lighting re-keyed for a dark stage — designed so a recruiter toggling the theme sees craft in both. **Reasoning recorded for the architect:** light-canonical is the recommendation; if the architect finds the configurator's studio lighting or the WebGL environment reads materially better dark-canonical, that is an ADR-able reversal — but the default ships **light**.
- **Type:** a precise, technological sans for UI and body (variable font preferred so weight/width can be used with intent — references the `font-variation-settings` note in `docs/inspirations.md`), optionally paired with a tighter display cut for the **APEX** wordmark and section headers. Type is a primary design element (Linear / Vercel / Klim references), set with the confident hierarchy those references demonstrate — not labels on boxes.
- **Texture / surface:** the surface language is _precision_, not grain — fine hairlines, the recurring thin accent "track line", subtle technological gridlines, soft studio shadows under the car (drei `ContactShadows`). No film grain (that is razors-edge's language). Any background effect stays CSS/SVG and inside the performance budget; the only GPU surface is the configurator canvas.
- **Photography / renders:** the static hero render, the fleet imagery, and the pre-baked configurator stills are all `next/image` with AVIF, art-directed (`<picture>` / `sizes`) for the cinematic crops. v1 uses **royalty-clear placeholder car photography / renders** (documented provenance, mirroring razors-edge) and the README notes the swap-for-real path. The pre-baked configurator render matrix (colour × wheel) is generated from the same placeholder model so it stays visually consistent with the live scene.

The designer-critic reviews every UI milestone against `docs/inspirations.md` with zero pochwał — see Phase 6.

## Reservation flow — mocked, but the centerpiece interaction

A polished multi-step wizard, entirely client-side against seeded deterministic mock data. It must feel real and delightful even though nothing persists. State in **Zustand** (the wizard machine); each step's form in **react-hook-form + Zod**; mock data fetched through **TanStack Query** against a thin in-memory mock data layer (with a small deterministic artificial latency so loading states, query keys, and the flow feel like they talk to a server). Validation schemas live in `src/lib/schemas/` and are the contract shared by the form steps and the mocked submit handler. The same frozen-clock discipline as razors-edge applies (a frozen `now` in `src/lib/clock.ts` so the date range, availability, and pricing are deterministic for tests and screenshots).

Steps:

1. **Pick a vehicle.** Grid/list of the fleet with daily price + key spec. Pre-selected if the user entered from a fleet card or from the configurator's "Reserve this configuration" (in which case the chosen **colour + wheels** are reflected in the summary — the spine thread). "Any available in tier" may be offered if the design supports it (decided at design phase — see Out of scope).
2. **Pick dates (range) + locations.** A **date-range picker** (from → to), constrained to the frozen-clock window (e.g. the next ~60 days), with a minimum/maximum rental length; plus **pickup** and **return** location selectors (from the mock locations; same-or-different pickup/return supported). Availability is generated deterministically from seeded mock data (each vehicle has mock pre-bookings / blackout ranges) so the range picker disables unavailable ranges in a stable, reproducible way. The number of rental days drives the price.
3. **Add extras.** GPS, child seat, additional driver, and **insurance tiers** (e.g. basic / plus / premium as a single-choice tier), each with a mock price (per-day or flat). Selections update the live price summary.
4. **Driver details.** Name, phone, email, driver's licence number (mock/validated format only — no real verification), optional notes — react-hook-form + Zod, inline accessible validation (labels + `aria-describedby` error wiring per `docs/conventions.md` § 5). A summary rail shows the running selection (vehicle + configured colour/wheels, date range + day count, locations, extras, insurance tier, and the **live price total**).
5. **Confirmation.** A mocked submit: the shared Zod schema validates, a thin server action returns a deterministic confirmation object (a booking reference, the full summary including the configured colour/wheels, an "add to calendar" `.ics` covering the rental range generated client-side, and an on-brand confirmation flourish). Nothing persists server-side; a reload starts fresh. The confirmation copy makes the mock nature graceful ("This is a demo reservation — no car was actually booked") without breaking the premium tone.

**Live price summary (cross-cutting, present from step 2 onward):** a pure, deterministic price function in `src/lib/` computes `dailyPrice × rentalDays + extras + insuranceTier (− any mock discount)` and is shown in the summary rail and the confirmation. This is the second piece of "domain logic" (alongside availability) that is pure, deterministic, and Vitest-tested in isolation — the same discipline as razors-edge's `getAvailability`.

Cross-cutting wizard requirements: a visible step indicator; back/next navigation that preserves entered data (Zustand, persisted to `localStorage` with a versioned key + TTL, mirroring razors-edge ADR-003 — the architect ratifies the exact persistence + the date-range-reconciliation rule on rehydrate in an ADR); deep-linkable entry (`/reserve?vehicle=…&color=…&wheels=…`) that pre-seeds the wizard from the configurator; full keyboard operability of the date-range picker and step controls; `prefers-reduced-motion`-respecting step transitions (GSAP/CSS per the no-Motion-by-default posture); and a mobile-first layout where the summary rail collapses into a sticky bottom bar at small widths.

## Mock data shape

All under `src/mocks/`, faker-driven, `faker.seed(<n>)` at the top of each factory module for determinism (`docs/conventions.md` § 6). Domain-coupled, never shared. Indicative shapes (final fields fixed when the Zod schemas are authored in Phase 3):

- **`Vehicle`** — `{ id, slug, name, tier: 'compact' | 'sedan' | 'suv' | 'performance', rangeKm, accel0to100, seats, dailyPriceMinor, currency, heroRenderSrc, available?: boolean }`. A fixed, hand-curated fleet (not random) so the fleet reads as a real line-up; faker fills only flavour copy. One vehicle is the **configurator hero** (the one with the GLB model + the colour/wheel options).
- **`ConfiguratorOption`** — the colour palette and wheel sets for the hero vehicle: `colors: { id, name, hex/material }[]` and `wheels: { id, name, previewSrc }[]`, plus the **pre-baked render matrix** mapping each `(colorId, wheelId)` to a static AVIF render (the Tier-3 fallback source). Fixed, hand-curated.
- **`Location`** — `{ id, slug, name, kind: 'airport' | 'city' | 'depot', address, lat, lng, staticMapSrc }`. Small fixed set; drives the pickup/return selectors and the locations section.
- **`Extra`** — `{ id, name, kind: 'gps' | 'child-seat' | 'additional-driver' | 'insurance', priceMinor, pricing: 'per-day' | 'flat', tier?: 'basic' | 'plus' | 'premium' }`. Fixed hand-curated set; insurance modelled as a single-choice tier among the extras (the architect fixes whether insurance is a separate field or an `Extra` subtype in the schema ADR).
- **`VehicleBooking` (mock pre-bookings / blackouts)** — `{ id, vehicleId, fromISODate, toISODate }`, a seeded set per vehicle so the date-range availability has realistic gaps. Deterministic from the seed.
- **`AvailabilityQuery` / availability result** — derived, not stored: a pure function `getRangeAvailability({ vehicleId, fromISODate, toISODate })` (and a helper that, for a vehicle, returns the disabled date ranges) composes blackouts + existing bookings against the requested range. Pure, deterministic, unit-tested in isolation (`src/lib/`).
- **`PriceQuote`** — derived: `priceQuote({ vehicle, rentalDays, extras, insuranceTier })` → `{ base, extrasTotal, insuranceTotal, total, currency }`. Pure, deterministic, unit-tested.
- **`Testimonial`** — `{ id, author, rating, quote, vehicle?: string }`, seeded set.
- **`ReservationDraft`** (wizard state, not mock data) — the Zustand store shape: `{ step, vehicleId?, config?: { colorId, wheelId }, range?: { fromISODate, toISODate }, pickupLocationId?, returnLocationId?, extras?: string[], insuranceTier?, driver?: { name, phone, email, licenceNo, notes? } }`. The submit payload is a Zod-validated projection of this.

The "current date" for availability is pinned to a deterministic frozen reference in `src/lib/clock.ts` (shared by tests and the UI), so screenshots and Playwright runs are reproducible and the date-range window never drifts — exactly razors-edge's frozen-clock discipline.

## Success criteria

All measurable; all gate v1 ship.

- **Lighthouse ≥ 95** in **all four** categories (Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, SEO) on the deployed demo, including the hero route (where the WebGL canvas is deferred behind the static-render LCP) and the `/reserve` flow. Measured via Lighthouse CI, median of 5 runs. The **mobile** Lighthouse run exercises the Tier-3 pre-baked-render fallback (the live WebGL scene does not load on the mid-tier-mobile profile) — this is how the budget is met honestly with a real 3D centrepiece.
- **Core Web Vitals green** on the hero: LCP < 2.5 s (the **static product render** is the LCP element — `priority` AVIF, correctly sized; the WebGL canvas loads after), CLS < 0.1 (the hero and the configurator section reserve their layout; the canvas mounts into a reserved box, no shift), INP < 200 ms (scroll handlers rAF-driven via GSAP; the WebGL render loop is on-demand / adaptive, no long tasks during scroll).
- **Configurator quality bar.** On a capable desktop GPU, the configurator orbits and swaps colour/wheels at a smooth 60 fps with no jank; colour and wheel swaps are visibly instant; the scene reads as a studio shot. The hero→configurator hand-off (ADR-004) is seamless (the live canvas reveals only when ready; no flash of an empty canvas or a layout jump). Degrades correctly: Tier-2 reduced-motion (no auto-orbit, user-driven only, crossfade hand-off), Tier-3 no-WebGL / mid-mobile (pre-baked-render swatches), Tier-4 no-JS (static composed frame). Designer-critic signs off against **Bruno Simon** + **Stripe/Vercel ship pages** with zero pochwał.
- **Reservation flow completes** end to end: enter from a fleet card or the configurator → vehicle (with carried-over config) → date range + locations from the availability picker → extras + insurance → driver details → confirmation with a booking reference and a working client-side `.ics`, in under 90 seconds of unhurried interaction, fully keyboard-operable, with deterministic availability + pricing across reloads.
- **Responsive from 320 px upward.** Every section, the configurator (live on capable devices, pre-baked fallback on mid-mobile), and the entire reservation flow are usable and on-brand at 320 px, tested up through 430 px mobile, tablet, and desktop. The gallery collapses to a vertical stack on mobile; the wizard summary rail collapses to a sticky bottom bar.
- **Theming.** Light + dark via CSS variables + `next-themes`, **light default**, `prefers-color-scheme` respected, no FOUC. The dark theme is an intentional "night drive / charging-bay" register, reviewed in its own right; the configurator's studio lighting is re-keyed (not just inverted) for the dark stage.
- **Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA.** Full keyboard navigation across nav, gallery, fleet selection, the configurator swatch controls, and the entire reservation wizard (the date-range picker is a proper keyboard-operable widget; the configurator colour/wheel controls are real DOM radios/buttons with `aria` state, not canvas-only). The WebGL canvas has an accessible text alternative (the configured car is described in real DOM — name + colour + wheels — so a screen-reader user gets the same information; the configurator is operable via the DOM swatch controls without needing to manipulate the 3D scene). Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 in both themes (the EV accent verified, not assumed). `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured at every animated site and in the configurator (no auto-orbit). Visible focus states styled to the brand, never removed. The hero conveys its content to screen readers regardless of WebGL or animation.
- **SEO.** Per-page meta + Open Graph, a designed OG image, `AutoRental` (schema.org) JSON-LD with the business, locations, and a price range, `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`. This is a local-business / rental site — the structured data is part of the fiction's credibility and a real differentiator vs a template.
- **Security baseline.** CSP headers configured and **verified compatible with R3F/three.js _and_ GSAP** — strict CSP, no reliance on `unsafe-eval` if achievable (the meld + razors-edge lesson; three.js shader compilation is GPU, not JS `eval`, but the architect verifies the bundle does not pull an `eval`/`new Function` path, exactly as razors-edge had to neutralise faker + zod's JIT). `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` is expected (Next/Tailwind + GSAP inline transforms; R3F also writes inline canvas styles). Input sanitization on the reservation form. No secrets; `.env.example` committed if any env is introduced. No real PII is collected or stored — the form is mocked and the README says so.
- **Tests.** Vitest unit coverage on the two pure domain pieces (`getRangeAvailability` and `priceQuote`) and the wizard + configurator state machines; Playwright E2E on the reservation happy path, the configurator swatch interaction (DOM controls + the Tier-3 pre-baked fallback path), and the `prefers-reduced-motion` hero behaviour; Lighthouse CI in the pipeline (desktop + the mobile fallback profile).

## Out of scope (v1)

Locked. v2 candidates only.

- **Any real backend, database, or persistence.** The reservation is mocked end to end; a reload starts fresh. (The web-only thesis — see DECISIONS.md ADR-001.) A "wire a real rental API (the unused Fastify slot, or NestJS)" path is noted in the README as v2.
- **Real authentication / accounts / "my reservations" history / real driver-licence verification.** No auth and no real identity checks in v1; licence is format-validated only.
- **Real payments / deposits.** The price is displayed; nothing is charged.
- **Real email / SMS confirmations.** The confirmation is on-screen + a client-side `.ics` only; the copy says so gracefully.
- **A live, interactive map embed** (Google Maps JS, Mapbox GL). v1 uses styled static-map treatments + maps click-through to protect the performance budget. A live embed is a v2 candidate behind a lazy boundary.
- **A fully configurable fleet (every car gets a 3D configurator).** v1 ships **one** configurable hero vehicle with the live R3F scene + the pre-baked matrix; the rest of the fleet uses static renders. Per-vehicle configurators are a v2 path.
- **A large colour/wheel matrix.** The pre-baked render matrix is the Tier-3 fallback, so the matrix is kept small (a handful of colours × 2–3 wheel sets) to keep the render count and asset weight reasonable. A larger matrix is v2.
- **Real-time, server-backed availability / dynamic pricing / fleet management.** Availability + pricing are pure deterministic functions over seeded mock data.
- **A real, IP-cleared manufacturer car model.** v1 uses a royalty-clear placeholder/concept model (no manufacturer trademark exposure); swapping in a licensed/commissioned brand model is the documented v2 path.
- **Club-GreenSock-only GSAP plugins.** v1 assumes free GSAP core + ScrollTrigger only; a Club plugin requires its own ADR.
- **Motion (the library) as a third animation dependency.** Not included by default; admitted only by an explicit bounded ADR if CSS + GSAP genuinely cannot express a needed wizard transition (CLAUDE.md § 5).
- **Internationalisation / multi-language.** English only (per CLAUDE.md § 2 and v1 scope).
- **Loyalty program, subscriptions, gift cards, blog/editorial content, multi-currency.** Brand-plausible future surfaces, not v1.
