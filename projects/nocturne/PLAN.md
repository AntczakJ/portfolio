# nocturne — PLAN

> **nocturne** is a web-only, GPU-accelerated, audio-reactive generative particle experience — a living field of hundreds of thousands of particles flowing through an animated curl-noise field, reacting in real time to music, the pointer, and curated presets, rendered with cinematic post-processing (bloom, vignette, subtle chromatic aberration) in a fullscreen, sovereign-dark cinematic UI.
> Brand display: **NOCTURNE**. Repo dir: `nocturne`. Slot 8. Dev port **3100**.
> Structural foundation: this project inherits the portfolio's proven web-only Next 15 posture (same per-project file discipline, sovereign-token policy, strict-CSP discipline, no-JS/degradation discipline) and the **R3F + drei integration patterns established by `apex`** (the `next/dynamic ssr:false` Canvas, Suspense + `<Loader>`, low `'use client'` boundary, capped `dpr`, adaptive quality, device-tier gating). It diverges from apex on exactly one load-bearing axis — apex's R3F is a **product configurator** (a GLB car, orbit, material swaps); nocturne's R3F is **abstract generative GPGPU art** (FBO ping-pong compute-in-fragment-shaders + audio-reactive custom GLSL). The re-use justification (the § 14 "no sibling re-skin" gate) is the spine of ADR-001 and is argued in full below.

## Problem

The portfolio demonstrates range across api-heavy backends, marketing craft, scroll storytelling, and one genuine 3D product surface (apex's configurator). What it does **not** yet have is a piece of pure **creative-coding / generative-graphics** craft — the kind of work that proves the author can write **custom GLSL**, run a **GPGPU simulation** on the GPU (not just render a supplied model), drive it from a **real-time audio analysis** pipeline, and hold **60 fps at hundreds of thousands of particles** while keeping the surrounding product (the UI chrome, the accessibility, the SEO, the no-WebGL floor) at the portfolio's production bar. That is a distinct and highly legible signal to the audience that judges frontend + WebGL + creative-coding depth, and it is the gap `nocturne` closes. apex proved "I can put a real, performant 3D _product_ on a marketing page." nocturne proves "I can write the shaders and the simulation myself, make them breathe with sound, and still ship it to the same quality bar." It is the most visually arresting piece in the portfolio by design — the one that holds a recruiter for five seconds before they have read a word.

## Audience

Single primary audience, judged in the first five seconds, tuned for it without apology:

**The recruiter / engineer / creative technologist judging frontend + WebGL + creative-coding craft.** Lands on the deployed demo URL from a portfolio index (`atrium`), a shared link, or an Awwwards-style roundup. Desktop monitor (1440 px+, 1920 px common), capable discrete or modern integrated GPU, headphones or speakers likely on (this is an audio piece). They _will_ open DevTools to check whether the "particles" are a real GPGPU simulation or a canned video / a few thousand `Points`; they _will_ check the frame rate; they _may_ throttle to test the tiering claims; and they decide in 5–10 seconds whether this is "another particle demo" or "someone who can write a curl-noise advection sim in GLSL, wire a Web Audio FFT into the turbulence and the bloom, and ship it accessibly." The wow must land **on first paint with sound on** and survive scrutiny under the hood.

**Secondary, never sacrificed:** the same person on **mobile** (a portfolio link tapped from a phone — common from a bio link or a roundup), where a 1M-particle 60 fps scene is not guaranteed. Mobile gets a genuinely lower tier (fewer particles, lower resolution scale, touch interaction, no audio-violent auto-motion by default), no horizontal overflow from 320 px, and — if WebGL is unavailable or the device is too weak — the same composed **poster-frame fallback** that the no-WebGL audience gets. And the **screen-reader / keyboard / reduced-motion user**, for whom the audio-reactive canvas is decorative (`aria-hidden`) with a real text alternative, the chrome is fully operable, and reduced-motion replaces audio-violent motion with a calm auto-drift or a still. The art direction is tuned for the primary audience; the tiering, the touch model, the no-WebGL floor, and the a11y are tuned for the rest — and neither is sacrificed.

## Wow moment (the five-second hold) — detailed spec

**THE WOW: a GPU particle flow-field that comes alive and breathes with the music.**

A field of **100k–1M particles** (the count is a function of the device tier — see Performance tiers) is advected in real time through an **animated curl-noise vector field**, the whole system simulated on the GPU via **GPGPU / FBO ping-pong**, and **driven by a Web Audio FFT** so the entire field _pulses to the sound_: the bass swells the turbulence and the particle energy, the highs shimmer the color and the trails, and the overall loudness pushes the **bloom** so the field literally brightens and blooms on the beat. The pointer attracts or repels a local region of the field, leaving a wake. Switching **presets** dramatically re-skins the whole look. It is cinematic, fullscreen, and sovereign-dark, with bloom, vignette, and a subtle chromatic aberration finishing it like a film frame.

The concrete technical anatomy (the architect ratifies the exact mechanism in ADR-002; this is the spec the architect builds the contract against):

- **GPGPU simulation, FBO ping-pong.** Particle **position** and **velocity** are stored in floating-point **textures** (a data texture per attribute, e.g. an N×N RGBA float/half-float FBO encoding XYZ + extra). Each frame, a **simulation fragment shader** reads the current position+velocity textures and writes the next ones into an off-screen framebuffer; the read/write targets **ping-pong** between two FBOs so the GPU never reads and writes the same texture in one pass. This is "compute in a fragment shader" — the whole sim runs on the GPU, the CPU only orchestrates. (Likely realised via three.js `GPUComputationRenderer` or an equivalent hand-rolled ping-pong; the architect picks and pins it.) **No per-particle CPU work** — that is what makes 1M particles at 60 fps possible and is the thing a reviewer checks for.
- **Curl-noise advection.** The velocity update samples a **curl of a noise field** (curl-noise / divergence-free noise — simplex/Perlin-derived) so particles flow in smooth, incompressible, swirling streams rather than scattering randomly. Flow **scale**, **speed**, and **turbulence** are uniforms — and they are exactly what the audio and the presets drive. A small amount of per-particle lifetime / re-spawn keeps the field from collapsing into the attractors.
- **Audio reactivity (the thing that makes it _breathe_).** A **Web Audio `AnalyserNode`** produces an FFT each frame; the frequency bins are reduced to a few **bands** (e.g. sub-bass / bass / mid / high) plus an overall **RMS/energy** envelope (smoothed with attack/decay so it pulses musically, not jittery). These feed the sim and the render as uniforms: **bass → turbulence + flow energy** (the field surges on the kick), **mids → particle motion / spread**, **highs → color shift + sparkle / trail intensity**, **overall energy → bloom strength + vignette breathing**. The result is a field that visibly _pulses to the music_ — the five-second hold is this exact moment: sound on, the field surges and blooms on the first beat.
- **Pointer interaction.** The pointer position (and a velocity-derived strength) is a uniform that creates a **local attractor or repeller** in the velocity field — the field bends toward or away from the cursor and leaves a wake, so the piece feels _responsive_, not a passive screensaver. Touch maps the same on mobile.
- **Presets (the dramatic re-skin).** A curated set of presets each define a coherent **look**: a **palette** (the color ramp the particle energy maps through), the **flow** (noise scale, speed, turbulence base), the **particle** treatment (size, opacity, additive vs alpha blending, trail length / feedback), and the **post-processing** mix (bloom threshold/strength, vignette, chromatic-aberration amount). Switching presets **cross-fades** the uniforms so the whole field morphs from one identity to another — e.g. from a cold, sparse, glacial drift to a hot, dense, molten swirl — in front of the viewer. Presets are the project's "many looks from one engine" proof and the second hold after the audio one.
- **Cinematic post-processing.** Rendered through **@react-three/postprocessing** (an `EffectComposer`): **bloom** (the audio-pulsed glow), **vignette** (the cinematic frame), and a **subtle chromatic aberration** (the film-lens finish). The render particle shader itself does the additive/soft-particle look (point sprites or instanced quads with a soft radial falloff; optional velocity-stretched trails). Post is tuned per preset and modulated by the audio energy.

**Audio sources (so the wow works for everyone who arrives):** a built-in **royalty-free / CC0 track** (sourced + credited, mirroring apex's CC0-model provenance discipline) that **autoplays-on-gesture** (browser autoplay policy means a single "tap to begin / unmute" gate — itself part of the cinematic intro), plus **microphone input** (live reactivity to the room — a strong demo move) and **user file upload** (drop your own track) as alternative sources. Provenance + licensing of the built-in track is tracked in `AGENT_NOTES.md` + README + a `CREDITS` line.

**Reference points** from `docs/inspirations.md`, named so the `designer-critic` judges against them with zero pochwał: the WebGL ambition and "what is possible in a browser" bar is judged against **Bruno Simon** (the canonical three.js-in-a-portfolio reference) and against **Codrops / Awwwards SOTD** (the technique + current-bar references — GPGPU particle fields and audio-reactive shaders are a Codrops staple, and the piece must clear that bar, not merely resemble it); the **cinematic restraint** of the chrome — a fullscreen canvas with a minimal HUD that never fights the art — is judged against **Linear** and **Vercel** (dark surfaces, deliberate accents, restraint with energy) and **Stripe** (custom WebGL that justifies its bundle cost); the **type** of the wordmark/intro and the preset labels is judged against **Klim** and **Linear** as specimens. The motion timing of the preset cross-fade and the HUD reveals is judged against **Linear**'s micro-interaction bar (e.g. a preset morph at ~600–900 ms on a designed easing, not a linear snap).

**The wow is additive over a floor that always works** (the four-tier discipline, mirroring apex, adapted for a generative piece):

- **Tier 1 — full (capable GPU, JS on, audio gesture given, motion OK):** the full 1M-ish GPGPU field, live audio reactivity (built-in track / mic / upload), pointer interaction, preset cross-fades, full post-processing.
- **Tier 2 — `prefers-reduced-motion`:** **no audio-violent motion.** The field defaults to a **calm auto-drift** (gentle curl flow, no beat-driven surges, muted/disabled audio reactivity, reduced bloom pulsing) **or a still composed frame**, at the user's effective preference. Presets still re-skin (it is a look change, not violent motion); pointer interaction is gentle or disabled. The decision between "calm drift" and "still" is pinned in ADR-004.
- **Tier 3 — mobile / mid-low GPU:** a genuinely lower tier — fewer particles (e.g. 50k–150k), lower **resolution scale** (render-target downscale), simpler/optional post, touch interaction. Still alive and audio-reactive where the device allows; degrades particle count + DPR + post under a `PerformanceMonitor`, never janks.
- **Tier 4 — no WebGL (failed/blocked WebGL2 context, no float-texture support, very low-end) or no-JS:** a **statically composed poster frame** (a pre-rendered AVIF still of the field at its most beautiful, the LCP-grade hero image) **plus the directory of presets** (each preset shown as a labelled still / card), the wordmark, the positioning copy, the credits, and the about content — all real server-rendered DOM. The piece still _reads_ and communicates what it is; WebGL and audio only ever **enhance** a page that already works and is legible to crawlers and screen readers.

The poster-frame + preset-directory fallback (Tier 4) is a **first-class deliverable**, not a stub — it is also the SEO/social surface and the screen-reader's view of the piece.

## Stack flavour

**`web-only`.**

This is an explicit, owner-confirmed decision and it is correct against `docs/conventions.md` § 10. **None of the five api-heavy triggers fires:**

- **No WebSocket / SSE.** There is no long-lived bidirectional channel. The audio analysis, the simulation, and the rendering all run **entirely in the browser**. Nothing streams from a server.
- **No background jobs / queue / cron.** No server-side scheduled work of any kind.
- **API not consumed by a non-Next client.** There is no public API. The only "data" is a small static set of preset definitions + the credits, served by this Next.js app to itself.
- **No heavy auth flow.** No accounts, no sessions, no tokens, no auth at all.
- **No heavy domain logic worth isolating from the UI.** The only logic is the GLSL simulation (GPU, not a backend concern), the audio-band reduction (a small pure function, Vitest-testable in `src/lib/`), and the preset state/cross-fade machine (client state). None of it benefits from a separate backend service.

**The GPGPU simulation and the Web Audio pipeline do _not_ make this api-heavy.** Both are **client** concerns; every § 10 trigger is a **server** concern. A heavy-WebGL, heavy-Web-Audio project is a frontend-complexity signal (it sets the animation stack — ADR-002), not a backend signal — exactly as apex established for its configurator. Forcing a backend here would be ceremony, not need, violating CLAUDE.md § 3 in reverse.

**Portfolio-composition note (planner enforcement, `docs/conventions.md` § 12).** The constraint is **satisfied with wide margin and slot 8 being web-only does not threaten it.** Per root `PROGRESS.md`, the portfolio already has **four api-heavy projects across four distinct backends** — `tape` (Elysia/Bun), `meld` (Hono/Node), `pulse` (NestJS), `atlas` (Fastify) — which exhausts the § 11 backend axis (all four cutting-edge Node/Bun backends are demonstrated). The 2–3 api-heavy minimum and the ≥2-backends (here four) variance requirement are met several times over. No backend axis remains to fill, so a deliberate web-only creative piece in slot 8 is the right use of the slot and is fully § 12-compliant. This is recorded as ADR-001 so the next planning pass does not mistake it for drift. (Note: this is also the second web-only creative project after apex if one counts only the creative slots — but `atrium` slot 7, web-only, sits between them, and the backend story being _complete_ means there is no standing flag to discharge here, unlike apex's situation. The composition is healthy.)

## Animation / 3D stack — R3F + drei + @react-three/postprocessing (single library family), with custom GLSL

**Selected: R3F + drei + @react-three/postprocessing — one animation-library family (R3F), no GSAP, no Motion** — per `docs/conventions.md` § 15 and CLAUDE.md § 5.

- **React Three Fiber + drei** own the entire visual surface: the WebGL2 context, the GPGPU compute pass (FBO ping-pong via three.js `GPUComputationRenderer` or a hand-rolled equivalent), the render particle system (instanced / `Points`), the camera, and the orchestration of the per-frame uniforms (audio bands, pointer, preset cross-fade). This is precisely the § 15 case for R3F: "only when the project actually uses WebGL / 3D" — this is **genuine GPU 3D + custom compute**, the strongest possible justification, not a "fancy 2D background."
- **@react-three/postprocessing** provides the `EffectComposer` for the cinematic finish (bloom, vignette, chromatic aberration). It is part of the R3F family (it wraps `postprocessing` for R3F), so it is **not** a second animation library — it is the post stack of the one library family the project committed to. (Recorded explicitly so the "do not stack three" rule is satisfied: this is one family — R3F + drei + r3f-postprocessing — not R3F + GSAP + Motion.)
- **Custom GLSL** is the heart: the **simulation shader** (curl-noise advection of position/velocity in the FBO ping-pong), the **render particle shader** (soft additive point sprites / instanced quads, color from the audio-mapped energy through the preset palette, optional velocity trails), and any **noise/utility** GLSL. These are hand-written, not a black-box library effect — that is the craft signal.
- **No GSAP, no Motion.** The preset cross-fades, the audio envelopes, and the pointer wake are all **uniform interpolations inside the R3F render loop** (lerp/spring on uniforms per frame) — not a DOM animation library's job. The minimal HUD's small UI transitions (panel reveals, preset-label changes) are **CSS transitions / `data-state`** on real DOM — also not requiring an animation library. So the project stays honestly inside one animation-library family. If, during the HUD build, a small React-state transition proves genuinely awkward in CSS, the frontend-engineer must **raise it in `AGENT_NOTES.md` for the architect to ratify in an ADR** — Motion is admitted only by an explicit, bounded ADR, and the default is **not** to include it.

The R3F-vs-DOM boundary: **R3F owns everything inside the canvas (sim, render, post, uniforms, camera). The DOM owns the minimal HUD chrome (controls, preset picker, audio-source picker, about/credits) with CSS transitions.** The one coupling point — the audio gesture gate and the audio-source switching, which live in the DOM but drive the canvas — is pinned in ADR-003 (the audio pipeline contract).

Licensing: R3F, drei, three.js, `postprocessing`, and `@react-three/postprocessing` are MIT. The built-in audio track is CC0 / royalty-free with documented provenance.

## Performance strategy (load-bearing — protects the 60 fps claim and Lighthouse on the non-canvas surfaces)

The 1M-particle 60 fps claim and the Lighthouse ≥ 95 claim on the non-canvas surfaces are both spec, and the GPGPU + Web Audio + post stack is the single biggest threat to both. The strategy is part of the spec; the architect pins the exact numbers and gates in ADR-002:

- **The simulation is 100% GPU.** Zero per-particle CPU work; the CPU only sets a handful of uniforms per frame. This is the load-bearing reason 1M particles can hit 60 fps and is what a reviewer verifies (no per-particle JS loops).
- **WebGL2 + float/half-float textures, capability-gated.** The sim requires WebGL2 (or WebGL1 + the float-texture extensions) and render-to-float-texture support. A **capability gate** (probe WebGL2 + the needed float/half-float render-target support at boot) routes unsupported clients straight to the **Tier-4 poster-frame fallback** — never a broken canvas. Half-float (`RGBA16F`) is preferred over full float where supported, for memory + bandwidth.
- **Performance tiers via particle count + resolution scale.** The particle count is the **simulation FBO dimension** (an N×N texture → N² particles; e.g. 256² ≈ 65k, 512² ≈ 262k, 1024² ≈ 1.05M). The tier picks N by **device capability** (a heuristic at boot — GPU hints, `devicePixelRatio`, mobile detection, an optional first-second FPS probe) and then **adapts at runtime** under a drei `PerformanceMonitor` / `AdaptiveDpr`: if FPS sags, drop the render DPR / post quality (and, if needed, the effective particle draw) before it janks. Documented tier table in the README, like apex's configurator measurement.
- **Capped DPR + on-demand where possible.** The render `Canvas` caps `dpr` (e.g. `[1, 2]`, lower on mobile). The render loop is **continuous** while audio-reactive (it must animate every frame), but **pauses** when the tab is hidden (`document.visibilityState`) and when reduced-motion selects a still — to spare battery and the main thread.
- **The canvas is code-split and deferred; the LCP is never the live canvas.** The R3F `Canvas`, three.js, the post stack, and the shaders are `next/dynamic`-imported with `ssr: false` inside a Suspense boundary with a `<Loader>` progress affordance, so three.js + the sim are **not in the initial JS bundle**. On first paint the route renders the **static poster frame** (AVIF, `priority` — the LCP element) and the intro/gesture gate **over** it; the live canvas mounts and reveals only once ready, so WebGL boot never blocks LCP and there is no CLS at the reveal. This is the apex hero→canvas hand-off pattern, reused for the poster→live-field reveal.
- **Audio analysis is cheap and rAF-aligned.** The `AnalyserNode` FFT is read once per frame on the render loop's rAF, reduced to a few bands by a small pure function — no extra timers, no main-thread audio DSP beyond the browser's native analyser.
- **Measured, not asserted.** The 60 fps claim is verified on a capable desktop GPU at the default tier and documented with the tier table; Lighthouse ≥ 95 is verified on the **non-canvas surfaces** (the about/credits route and the poster-frame fallback state) and the canvas route's perf is **measured separately and documented like apex's configurator**, because a continuously-animating audio-reactive WebGL canvas is not a fair Lighthouse-TBT subject and that is stated honestly.

## Design direction

Sovereign visual identity — **no token reuse from any sibling** (`docs/conventions.md` § 14; the § 14 "no sibling re-skin" gate is a hard review item — see ADR-001 and Phase 6). Built from scratch in `app/globals.css`.

- **Aesthetic: sovereign dark-cinematic.** A deep, near-black "nocturne" ground (a true cinematic dark — not razors-edge's brass-on-near-black dark-luxe, not atrium's six-hue lobby, not apex's "night drive" dark which is a re-key of a light-canonical product identity). nocturne is **dark-canonical and dark-native** — the dark is the stage for light: the particle field _is_ the color, glowing additively against the black, and the chrome recedes so the art is the whole screen. The accent system is **not a single fixed brand hue** but the **preset palette ramps themselves** — the identity is luminous, shifting, generative. The fixed chrome (HUD text, controls, focus rings) uses a restrained near-white / cool-grey ink that holds contrast against the dark without competing with the field.
- **Dark is canonical and the default.** Per CLAUDE.md § 4 the site still ships **light + dark** via CSS variables + `next-themes` and respects `prefers-color-scheme` — but for nocturne **dark is the canonical, default, and design-true theme** (a cinematic audio-visual piece is a dark-room experience; light is the deliberate departure). The **light theme applies only to the chrome and the non-canvas surfaces** (the about/credits page, the no-WebGL poster-frame surround) — a clean light reading mode for that content — while the **canvas itself remains a dark cinematic stage in both themes** (you do not "light-mode" a fireworks display; the field needs the black to glow). This light/dark split (chrome themes, stage stays dark) is reasoned for the architect and pinned in an ADR; the default ships **dark**.
- **The minimal cinematic HUD (the UI shape).** The chrome is a **fullscreen canvas with a minimal HUD overlay** that never fights the art: a small wordmark (corner), a **preset picker** (a compact, keyboard-operable strip / menu of the curated looks with labels), an **audio-source picker** (built-in track / mic / upload, with a play/mute + a tasteful level/spectrum affordance), a small **controls** affordance (the few exposed parameters — e.g. an intensity / "calm" toggle, pointer-interaction toggle), and an unobtrusive **about / credits** entry. The HUD is **auto-dimming** (recedes after a few seconds of inactivity, returns on pointer move / focus) so the art owns the screen — but it is always reachable by keyboard and never hidden from assistive tech. Restraint judged against **Linear** + **Vercel**; it must feel like a designed instrument, not a debug panel.
- **Type:** a precise, technological/display sans for the **NOCTURNE** wordmark and the preset/source labels (variable font preferred so weight can be used with intent — references the `font-variation-settings` note in `docs/inspirations.md`), loaded via `next/font`, OFL or equivalently clear license (documented like apex's font provenance). Type is a primary element — the wordmark and the preset names are a specimen (Klim / Linear references), not labels on boxes.
- **Texture / surface:** the surface language is **light and glow**, not grain — the field's additive bloom, soft particle falloff, and the post-processing vignette/aberration are the texture. The HUD uses fine hairlines + subtle translucency (a faint dark scrim behind text for legibility over a bright field) — the only GPU surface is the canvas; the HUD is CSS/DOM.
- **The poster frame + preset stills** are `next/image` AVIF, art-directed (the most beautiful frame of the field as the hero still + one labelled still per preset for the directory) — generated from the live engine so they stay visually faithful.

The designer-critic reviews every UI milestone against `docs/inspirations.md` with zero pochwał — see Phase 6 — and runs the **§ 14 no-sibling-re-skin gate explicitly against apex** (the only other R3F project): different motif (abstract generative vs product configurator), different palette (generative-luminous dark-native vs light-canonical EV product), different interaction model (audio + pointer field vs orbit + material swatches). This must PASS in review.

## Audio-source model

Three sources, one analysis pipeline. The architect pins the Web Audio graph + the autoplay-gesture contract + the analysis-band reduction in ADR-003.

- **Built-in track (default).** A **royalty-free / CC0** track, bundled (or streamed from the app's own static assets), credited in `AGENT_NOTES.md` + README + an in-UI credits line (provenance discipline mirroring apex's CC0 model). Because browser **autoplay policy** blocks audio before a user gesture, the cinematic intro doubles as the gesture gate: a "tap / press to begin" (or "unmute") affordance that, on activation, resumes the `AudioContext`, starts the track, and lets the field surge alive — making the autoplay constraint a designed moment rather than a wart.
- **Microphone input.** With explicit user permission (a clear, accessible prompt + a permission-denied graceful path), the live mic feeds the analyser — the field reacts to the room / the user's voice / ambient music. A strong demo move; gated behind the permission and clearly indicated when live.
- **File upload.** The user drops/selects a local audio file (decoded via the Web Audio API into a buffer source or an `<audio>` element through a `MediaElementAudioSourceNode`); the field reacts to their own track. No upload leaves the browser — stated for privacy in the README.

One **`AnalyserNode`** sits at the analysis point regardless of source; a small **pure band-reduction function** (FFT bins → sub-bass / bass / mid / high + smoothed RMS energy, with attack/decay smoothing) lives in `src/lib/` and is **Vitest-tested in isolation**. The bands are the uniforms the sim + render + post consume. Source switching swaps the upstream node into the same analyser without rebuilding the graph.

## Site IA

Deliberately minimal — this is an immersive single-experience piece, not a multi-section marketing site.

1. **`/` — the immersive canvas route (the whole experience).** First paint: the **static poster frame** (LCP) + the cinematic intro / **audio gesture gate** (wordmark, one line of positioning, "press to begin"). On gesture: the live GPGPU field surges alive with the built-in track, the minimal HUD fades in (auto-dimming), pointer interaction live, presets and audio-source switching available. This single route _is_ the product. Under no-WebGL / no-JS it renders the poster frame + the preset directory + the wordmark/positioning as real DOM (Tier 4).
2. **`/about` (and/or a credits surface)** — a clean, light-readable **non-canvas** surface: what nocturne is, the technique (GPGPU curl-noise + Web Audio FFT, in plain language for the audience that wants the story), the **preset directory** (each preset as a labelled still + one line describing its look — this is also the no-WebGL directory), the **credits** (the CC0 track + provenance, the fonts, the libraries), the accessibility statement (the audio-reactive canvas is decorative with this text alternative; reduced-motion behaviour; keyboard operation), and a link back to the experience and to the portfolio (`atrium`). This is the surface Lighthouse ≥ 95 is measured on and a key part of the SEO/legibility floor.

A persistent, accessible affordance lets the user reach `/about`, toggle the chrome theme, mute/unmute, and switch presets/sources from within the experience — all keyboard-reachable.

## Success criteria

All measurable; all gate v1 ship.

- **The wow holds 60 fps** on a capable desktop GPU at the **default tier**, with the live audio-reactive field + presets + post-processing, no jank during preset cross-fades or pointer interaction. Verified and documented with the tier table (count × resolution scale per tier), like apex's configurator measurement.
- **Performance tiers with graceful degradation.** A documented tier table maps device capability → particle count (sim FBO size) + render resolution scale + post quality, with a runtime `PerformanceMonitor` that adapts (drops DPR / post / effective count) **before** it janks. Mid/low GPUs and mobile get a genuinely lower, still-alive tier; the unsupported floor is Tier 4.
- **No-WebGL fallback** — a **statically composed poster frame** (AVIF, LCP-grade) **+ the preset directory** (labelled stills) **+ the wordmark/positioning/credits**, all real server-rendered DOM, shown when WebGL2 / float-render-target support is absent or JS is off. First-class deliverable, not a stub.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` respected** — no audio-violent motion: a calm auto-drift (gentle curl flow, muted audio reactivity, reduced bloom pulsing) **or** a still composed frame (the exact choice pinned in ADR-004). Presets may still re-skin (a look change, not violent motion); pointer wake gentle/off. No autoplaying audio under reduced-motion without explicit user start.
- **WCAG 2.2 AA on the UI chrome** — the controls, preset picker, and audio-source picker are fully **keyboard-operable** (real DOM controls with `aria` state — radios/buttons/menus, not canvas-only), visible brand-styled focus states, contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for all chrome text/icons against their (scrimmed) backdrop in both chrome themes. The auto-dimming HUD never becomes keyboard-unreachable or hidden from assistive tech.
- **The audio-reactive canvas is `aria-hidden` decorative** with an **accessible text alternative** — a real-DOM description of what the piece is and what is currently playing/selected (preset name, audio source), so a screen-reader user gets the meaning without the GPU scene. - **Lighthouse ≥ 95 on the non-canvas surfaces** (the `/about`/credits route and the poster-frame fallback state) across all four categories, median of 5 runs. **The canvas route's performance is measured separately and documented** (like apex's configurator) — a continuously-animating audio-reactive WebGL canvas is not a fair Lighthouse-TBT subject, stated honestly; its 60 fps + tiering are the relevant metrics there.
- **Core Web Vitals on first paint:** LCP < 2.5 s (the **static poster frame** is the LCP element — `priority` AVIF; the live canvas mounts after), CLS < 0.1 (the canvas mounts into a reserved fullscreen box; the HUD reveal does not shift layout), no long-task storms before the gesture gate (the canvas/three.js are code-split and load behind the poster).
- **CSP posture for WebGL (and any WASM) defined** — strict CSP verified compatible with three.js + R3F + the post stack + Web Audio (the meld/razors-edge/apex lesson: three.js shader compilation is GPU, not JS `eval`; the architect verifies the bundle pulls no `eval`/`new Function` path; if any decode/worker path needs `wasm-unsafe-eval` or a `worker-src`/`blob:` allowance, it is declared and justified in the CSP ADR). `media-src`/`connect-src` cover the bundled track + mic + uploaded-file object URLs. No secrets; `.env.example` if any env is introduced.
- **SEO surface** — per-page meta + Open Graph, a designed **OG image** (the poster frame), JSON-LD (`CreativeWork`/`WebSite` as fits an art piece), `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`. The `/about` content + the poster-frame DOM make the piece crawlable and legible despite the WebGL.
- **Mobile behaviour** — lower tier (fewer particles, lower resolution scale) + **touch interaction** (touch attractor/repeller), **no horizontal overflow from 320 px**, the HUD reflows to a thumb-reachable layout, audio gesture gate works on touch, and the Tier-4 poster fallback covers devices that cannot run the sim. Tested 320 px upward.
- **Theming.** Light + dark chrome via CSS variables + `next-themes`, **dark default/canonical**, `prefers-color-scheme` respected, no FOUC; the **canvas stage stays dark in both chrome themes** (reasoned; pinned in an ADR).
- **Security baseline.** Strict CSP (above); mic permission handled with explicit consent + graceful denial; uploaded audio never leaves the browser (object URL only); input sanitisation on any text input; no real PII.
- **Tests.** Vitest unit on the pure **audio band-reduction** function (FFT-bin → bands + smoothed RMS, attack/decay envelope determinism) and the **preset cross-fade / state machine** (uniform-target interpolation, preset switching, reduced-motion branch selection, capability-gate routing). Playwright E2E on the chrome critical paths that do not require a real GPU: the intro/gesture gate → HUD reveal, the preset picker (keyboard + pointer), the audio-source picker (built-in/mic-permission-mock/upload-accept), the `/about` route, the theme toggle, the `prefers-reduced-motion` path (calm/still, no audio-violent motion), and the no-WebGL fallback (poster + directory render). Lighthouse CI on the non-canvas surfaces.

## Tasks

Ordered, granular, each sized `S` / `M` / `L` with the responsible subagent. The implement phase starts after the architect ratifies the Phase-0 ADRs (the GPGPU architecture + R3F/Next integration + perf tiers; the audio pipeline + autoplay-gesture contract; the reduced-motion + degradation + CSP contract). nocturne **inherits apex's proven R3F/Next integration patterns** (`next/dynamic ssr:false` Canvas, Suspense + `<Loader>`, low `'use client'` boundary, capped `dpr`, adaptive quality, device-tier/capability gating, the static-still→live-canvas hand-off, the strict-CSP-with-three.js verification), so Phase 0 focuses on the **new** surface: the GPGPU FBO ping-pong + custom GLSL, the Web Audio pipeline, the preset cross-fade engine, and the generative-piece degradation contract.

## Out of scope (v1)

Locked. v2 candidates only.

- **Any real backend, database, or persistence.** No server beyond the static Next app + the `/about` content + the deploy infra. (The web-only thesis — DECISIONS.md ADR-001.)
- **A large preset library.** v1 ships a small, curated, dramatically-distinct set (a handful). More presets — and user-savable / shareable presets via URL params — are a v2 path noted in the README.
- **Recording / export** (capturing the canvas to a video / GIF / image sequence in-app, "export your moment"). A strong v2 feature; out of v1 to protect the budget.
- **MIDI / external control** (Web MIDI to drive presets/params from a controller). v2.
- **Real authentication / accounts / saved galleries.** None in v1.
- **Streaming-service integration** (Spotify/SoundCloud audio sources). v1 audio is built-in track + mic + local file upload only; no third-party audio API.
- **A second, fully distinct simulation engine** (e.g. a fluid/SPH sim alongside the curl-noise field). v1 is one engine, many presets. A second engine is v2.
- **VR / WebXR.** Out of scope.
- **Internationalisation / multi-language.** English only (CLAUDE.md § 2 and v1 scope).
- **GSAP or Motion as additional animation libraries.** Not included; the R3F family + CSS transitions cover the project. Admitted only by an explicit bounded ADR (CLAUDE.md § 5).
- **Lighthouse-asserting the live canvas route.** The canvas perf is measured + documented (60 fps + tiers), not gated via Lighthouse, because a continuously-animating audio-reactive WebGL canvas is not a fair Lighthouse-TBT subject — stated honestly (the apex precedent).
